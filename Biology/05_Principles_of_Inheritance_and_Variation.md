# Chapter 05: Principles of Inheritance and Variation

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

### Mendel’s Laws of Inheritance
* **Gregor Mendel**: Father of genetics. Conducted hybridization experiments on **garden peas (Pisum sativum)** for 7 years (1856-1863).
* **Reasons for selecting Pea**: True-breeding varieties, contrasting traits, short life span, cross-pollination is easy to perform.
* Mendel selected 14 true-breeding pea plant varieties as pairs. Characters studied: Stem height (Tall/Dwarf), Flower colour (Violet/White), Flower position (Axial/Terminal), Pod shape (Inflated/Constricted), Pod colour (Green/Yellow), Seed shape (Round/Wrinkled), Seed colour (Yellow/Green).

### Inheritance of One Gene (Monohybrid Cross)
* Crossing of two plants differing in one character. F1 (first filial) generation showed only one parental trait. F2 generation showed both traits in a 3:1 ratio (Phenotypic) and 1:2:1 ratio (Genotypic).
* **Alleles**: Slightly different forms of the same gene.
* **Homozygous** (TT or tt) vs **Heterozygous** (Tt).
* **Test Cross**: Crossing F1 hybrid with the recessive parent to determine the genotype of the F1 individual. Ratio is 1:1.
* **Law of Dominance**: Characters are controlled by discrete units (factors). In a dissimilar pair, one dominates the other.
* **Law of Segregation**: Alleles do not blend. During gamete formation, alleles separate (segregate) so that each gamete receives only one of the two alleles.

### Exceptions / Extensions to Mendelian Principles
* **Incomplete Dominance**: The F1 phenotype is intermediate between the two parents. e.g., Flower colour in snapdragon (*Antirrhinum*) and Four o'clock plant (*Mirabilis jalapa*). Cross of Red (RR) $\times$ White (rr) $\rightarrow$ Pink (Rr). F2 Ratio: Genotypic 1:2:1, Phenotypic 1:2:1.
* **Co-dominance**: Both alleles express themselves equally in the F1 heterozygote. e.g., ABO blood grouping in humans (alleles $I^A, I^B, i$). $I^A$ and $I^B$ are co-dominant, yielding blood group AB.
* **Multiple Alleles**: More than two alleles govern a single character. e.g., ABO blood groups (3 alleles). Found only in a population, not in an individual.
* **Pleiotropy**: A single gene controls multiple phenotypic traits. e.g., Phenylketonuria, sickle cell anemia, starch synthesis in pea seeds (controls starch grain size and seed shape).
* **Polygenic Inheritance**: Traits controlled by three or more genes. Phenotype reflects the contribution of each allele (quantitative inheritance). e.g., Human skin colour (A, B, C genes).

### Inheritance of Two Genes (Dihybrid Cross)
* Cross considering two characters simultaneously. F2 phenotypic ratio is 9:3:3:1.
* **Law of Independent Assortment**: When two pairs of traits are combined in a hybrid, segregation of one pair of characters is independent of the other pair.

### Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance
* Proposed by **Sutton and Boveri**. They noted that behavior of chromosomes parallels the behavior of genes.
* Chromosomes and genes both occur in pairs in diploid cells, segregate during gamete formation, and pair independently.
* **Thomas Hunt Morgan** proved this experimentally using fruit flies (**Drosophila melanogaster**).

### Linkage and Recombination
* **Linkage**: Physical association of genes on a chromosome. Linked genes do not assort independently.
* **Recombination**: Generation of non-parental gene combinations due to crossing over during meiosis.
* **Sturtevant** used the frequency of recombination to map gene positions on chromosomes. (1% recombination = 1 centiMorgan/map unit).

### Sex Determination
* **XX-XY type**: Humans, Drosophila. Males are heterogametic (XY), females homogametic (XX).
* **XX-XO type**: Grasshopper, some insects. Males are XO, females XX.
* **ZZ-ZW type**: Birds. Females are heterogametic (ZW), males homogametic (ZZ).
* **Haplo-diploidy**: Honey bees. Females are diploid (32, develop from fertilized eggs). Males (drones) are haploid (16, develop from unfertilized eggs via parthenogenesis).

### Mutation
* A phenomenon which results in alteration of DNA sequences and consequently results in changes in the genotype and phenotype.
* **Point Mutation**: Change in a single base pair (e.g., Sickle-cell anemia).
* **Frameshift Mutation**: Insertion or deletion of one or a few base pairs.

### Genetic Disorders
* **Pedigree Analysis**: Study of the inheritance of traits in several generations of a family.
* **Mendelian Disorders** (determined by alteration in a single gene):
    * **Haemophilia**: Sex-linked recessive. Blood fails to clot. Queen Victoria's pedigree shows this.
    * **Colour Blindness**: Sex-linked recessive. Inability to distinguish red and green colors. Defect in X chromosome.
    * **Sickle-cell Anemia**: Autosomal recessive. Substitution of Glutamic acid by Valine at the 6th position of the beta-globin chain. HbA mutates to HbS. RBCs become sickle-shaped under low oxygen.
    * **Phenylketonuria (PKU)**: Autosomal recessive. Lack of enzyme converting phenylalanine to tyrosine. Causes mental retardation.
    * **Thalassemia**: Autosomal recessive. Reduced synthesis of alpha or beta globin chains, causing anemia. It is a quantitative defect (unlike sickle-cell which is qualitative).
* **Chromosomal Disorders** (absence, excess, or abnormal arrangement of one or more chromosomes):
    * **Aneuploidy**: Gain or loss of a chromosome due to failure of segregation (nondisjunction).
    * **Down's Syndrome**: Trisomy of chromosome 21 (47, XX,+21 or 47, XY,+21). Symptoms: Short stature, round head, furrowed tongue, partially open mouth, broad palm with characteristic palm crease.
    * **Klinefelter's Syndrome**: Presence of an additional X chromosome in males (47, XXY). Overall masculine development but with feminine features (gynaecomastia). Sterile.
    * **Turner's Syndrome**: Absence of one X chromosome in females (45, XO). Sterile, rudimentary ovaries, lack of secondary sexual characters.

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### Dihybrid Cross Derivation (Yellow Round $\times$ Green Wrinkled)
1. **Parents**: YYRR (Yellow Round) $\times$ yyrr (Green Wrinkled)
2. **Gametes**: YR and yr
3. **F1 Generation**: YyRr (All Yellow Round)
4. **Selfing F1**: YyRr $\times$ YyRr
5. **Gametes from F1**: YR, Yr, yR, yr
6. **Punnett Square Results (F2)**:
   * 9 Yellow Round (Y_R_)
   * 3 Yellow Wrinkled (Y_rr)
   * 3 Green Round (yyR_)
   * 1 Green Wrinkled (yyrr)
*Phenotypic Ratio* = 9:3:3:1.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Trap Warnings
* **Thalassemia vs Sickle Cell**: Thalassemia is a quantitative problem (too few globin molecules synthesized). Sickle-cell is a qualitative problem (synthesized globin is defective).
* **Linkage and Recombination Frequency**: Recombination frequency can NEVER exceed 50%. A frequency of 50% means the genes are independently assorting.
* **Bird Sex Determination**: In birds, females are ZW (heterogametic), meaning the *female* determines the sex of the offspring, not the male.

### Distinction Table

| Feature | Mendelian Disorders | Chromosomal Disorders |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cause** | Mutation in a single gene | Alteration in chromosome number/structure |
| **Inheritance Pattern** | Follows Mendelian principles (can be traced in pedigree) | Generally random, rarely follows Mendelian rules (nondisjunction) |
| **Examples** | Haemophilia, Sickle-cell anemia | Down's syndrome, Klinefelter's syndrome |

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

**Problem 1 (Objective/NEET):** A colour-blind man marries a woman with normal sight who has no history of colour blindness in her family. What is the probability of their grandson being colour-blind?
**Speed Method:**
1. Man (X^c Y) $\times$ Woman (XX).
2. All daughters will be carriers (X^c X). All sons will be normal (XY).
3. Carrier daughter (X^c X) marries normal man (XY).
4. Her sons have a 50% chance of receiving X^c.
*Answer:* 50% (0.5).

**Problem 2 (Subjective/ISC):** Explain the chromosomal basis of sex determination in honey bees. (3 marks)
**Step-by-Step Method:**
1. Identify the system as **haplo-diploid sex determination**.
2. **Females** (Queens and Workers) develop from fertilized eggs (sperm + egg). Thus, they are diploid and have 32 chromosomes.
3. **Males** (Drones) develop from unfertilized eggs by **parthenogenesis**. Thus, they are haploid and have 16 chromosomes.
4. Note that males produce sperms by **mitosis**, not meiosis. Therefore, males do not have a father and cannot have sons, but have a grandfather and can have grandsons.
