# Chapter 5: Principles of Inheritance and Variation

## PART 1: Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

### 1. Introduction to Genetics
Genetics is the branch of biology that deals with the inheritance and variation of characters from parents to offspring.
- **Inheritance**: The process by which characters are passed on from parent to progeny; it is the basis of heredity.
- **Variation**: The degree by which progeny differ from their parents.
- **Gene**: The functional unit of inheritance, a segment of DNA that codes for a specific trait.
- **Allele**: Slightly different forms of the same gene, located at the same **locus** on homologous chromosomes.
- **Homozygous**: An individual having two identical alleles of a particular gene (e.g., TT or tt).
- **Heterozygous**: An individual having two different alleles of a particular gene (e.g., Tt).
- **Phenotype**: The observable morphological appearance or trait of an organism.
- **Genotype**: The genetic constitution of an organism.

### 2. Mendel's Laws of Inheritance
Gregor Mendel, the Father of Genetics, conducted hybridization experiments on garden peas (*Pisum sativum*) for seven years (1856-1863). 
Mendel selected **seven pairs of contrasting characters**: Stem height (Tall/Dwarf), Flower colour (Violet/White), Flower position (Axial/Terminal), Pod shape (Inflated/Constricted), Pod colour (Green/Yellow), Seed shape (Round/Wrinkled), and Seed colour (Yellow/Green).

#### Inheritance of One Gene (Monohybrid Cross)
A cross between two parents differing in a single pair of contrasting characters is called a monohybrid cross.
- **Law of Dominance**: 
  1. Characters are controlled by discrete units called **factors**.
  2. Factors occur in pairs.
  3. In a dissimilar pair of factors (heterozygous), one member of the pair dominates (dominant) the other (recessive).
- **Law of Segregation** (Purity of Gametes): Alleles do not blend. Both characters are recovered in the F2 generation. During gametogenesis, the factors or alleles of a pair segregate from each other such that a gamete receives only one of the two factors.

#### Inheritance of Two Genes (Dihybrid Cross)
A cross between two parents differing in two pairs of contrasting characters.
- **Law of Independent Assortment**: When two pairs of traits are combined in a hybrid, segregation of one pair of characters is independent of the other pair of characters.

### 3. Non-Mendelian Inheritance (Post-Mendelian Genetics)
- **Incomplete Dominance**: The F1 phenotype does not resemble either of the two parents and is in between the two. Example: Flower colour in *Antirrhinum majus* (Snapdragon) and *Mirabilis jalapa* (Four o'clock plant). Crossing red (RR) and white (rr) produces pink (Rr).
- **Co-dominance**: The F1 generation resembles both parents equally. Both alleles express themselves independently in heterozygotes. Example: ABO blood grouping in humans.
- **Multiple Alleles**: When more than two alleles govern the same character. They can be found only in a population study. Example: ABO blood groups (alleles $I^A$, $I^B$, $i$).
- **Pleiotropy**: A single gene can exhibit multiple phenotypic expressions. The underlying mechanism is often the effect of a gene on metabolic pathways. Example: Phenylketonuria, Sickle cell anemia, and starch synthesis in pea seeds.
- **Polygenic Inheritance**: Traits controlled by three or more genes. Phenotype reflects the contribution of each allele (quantitative inheritance). Example: Human skin colour.

### 4. Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance
Proposed by **Walter Sutton and Theodor Boveri**. They noted that the behavior of chromosomes was parallel to the behavior of genes and used chromosome movement to explain Mendel's laws.
- Chromosomes and genes both occur in pairs in diploid cells.
- Homologous chromosomes separate during meiosis, and so do the alleles.
- Fertilization restores the diploid number.
Thomas Hunt Morgan proved this theory using *Drosophila melanogaster* (fruit flies), noting they reproduce quickly and possess easily distinguishable contrasting traits.

### 5. Linkage and Recombination
- **Linkage**: The physical association of genes on a chromosome. Linked genes do not show independent assortment.
- **Recombination**: The generation of non-parental gene combinations due to crossing over during meiosis. 
- Morgan found that genes located on the same chromosome are inherited together (linkage) and recombinant frequencies depend on the physical distance between genes. **Tightly linked genes show very low recombination; loosely linked genes show higher recombination.**
- **Genetic Mapping**: Alfred Sturtevant used the frequency of recombination between gene pairs on the same chromosome as a measure of the distance between genes and 'mapped' their position on the chromosome (1% recombination = 1 centiMorgan/map unit).

### 6. Sex Determination
The establishment of the sex of an organism.
- **Male heterogamety**: Males produce two different types of gametes. 
  - **XO type**: In insects (grasshoppers), males have only one X chromosome (XO), females have XX.
  - **XY type**: In humans and *Drosophila*, males have X and Y chromosomes, females have XX.
- **Female heterogamety**: Females produce two different types of gametes.
  - **ZW type**: In birds, females have Z and W chromosomes, males have ZZ.
- **Sex Determination in Honey Bee**: Haplodiploid sex-determination system. Females are diploid (32 chromosomes) formed from fertilized eggs. Males (drones) are haploid (16 chromosomes) formed unfertilized eggs by **parthenogenesis**.

### 7. Mutation
A phenomenon which results in alteration of DNA sequences and consequently results in changes in the genotype and the phenotype of an organism.
- **Point Mutation**: Change in a single base pair of DNA (e.g., Sickle cell anemia).
- **Frameshift Mutation**: Deletion or insertion of base pairs resulting in the shifting of the reading frame.

### 8. Genetic Disorders
- **Pedigree Analysis**: The study of inheritance of traits in several generations of a family.
- **Mendelian Disorders**: Determined by alteration or mutation in a single gene. Transmitted according to Mendelian principles.
  - **Autosomal Dominant**: Myotonic dystrophy.
  - **Autosomal Recessive**: Sickle-cell anemia, Phenylketonuria, Thalassemia, Cystic fibrosis.
  - **X-linked Recessive**: Haemophilia, Colour blindness.
- **Chromosomal Disorders**: Caused by absence or excess or abnormal arrangement of one or more chromosomes.
  - **Aneuploidy**: Gain or loss of a chromosome(s) due to failure of segregation of chromatids during cell division (e.g., Down syndrome - Trisomy 21).
  - **Polyploidy**: Increase in a whole set of chromosomes, often seen in plants.
  - **Down's Syndrome**: Trisomy of 21st chromosome (45 + XY/XX).
  - **Klinefelter's Syndrome**: Additional copy of X chromosome in males (44 + XXY).
  - **Turner's Syndrome**: Absence of one X chromosome in females (44 + XO).
## PART 2: Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### 1. Monohybrid Cross and Punnett Square
A cross involving a single contrasting character.
- **Parents (P generation):** Pure Tall (TT) x Pure Dwarf (tt)
- **Gametes:** (T) and (t)
- **F1 Generation:** All Tt (Tall)
- **Selfing F1 (Tt x Tt):**
  - **Gametes:** (T), (t) x (T), (t)
- **Punnett Square (F2 Generation):**
| ♀ \ ♂ | T | t |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **T** | TT (Tall) | Tt (Tall) |
| **t** | Tt (Tall) | tt (Dwarf) |

**Derivation Results:**
- **Phenotypic Ratio:** 3 Tall : 1 Dwarf (3:1)
- **Genotypic Ratio:** 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt (1:2:1)

### 2. Dihybrid Cross and Punnett Square
A cross involving two pairs of contrasting characters (e.g., Seed shape and Seed colour).
- **Parents:** Pure Round Yellow (RRYY) x Pure Wrinkled Green (rryy)
- **Gametes:** (RY) and (ry)
- **F1 Generation:** RrYy (Round Yellow)
- **Selfing F1 (RrYy x RrYy):**
  - **Gametes for both:** (RY), (Ry), (rY), (ry)
- **Punnett Square (F2 Generation - 16 squares):**
| ♀ \ ♂ | RY | Ry | rY | ry |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **RY** | RRYY (Round, Yellow) | RRYy (Round, Yellow) | RrYY (Round, Yellow) | RrYy (Round, Yellow) |
| **Ry** | RRYy (Round, Yellow) | RRyy (Round, Green) | RrYy (Round, Yellow) | Rryy (Round, Green) |
| **rY** | RrYY (Round, Yellow) | RrYy (Round, Yellow) | rrYY (Wrinkled, Yellow) | rrYy (Wrinkled, Yellow) |
| **ry** | RrYy (Round, Yellow) | Rryy (Round, Green) | rrYy (Wrinkled, Yellow) | rryy (Wrinkled, Green) |

**Derivation Results:**
- **Phenotypic Ratio:** 9 Round Yellow : 3 Round Green : 3 Wrinkled Yellow : 1 Wrinkled Green (9:3:3:1)

### 3. The Test Cross Mechanism
A cross between an F1 individual (dominant phenotype, unknown genotype) and its homozygous recessive parent. It is used to determine the zygosity (homozygous or heterozygous) of the dominant individual.
- **Scenario A (Homozygous Dominant):** TT (Tall) x tt (Dwarf) -> All Tt (100% Tall). If all progeny are dominant, the unknown parent was homozygous.
- **Scenario B (Heterozygous):** Tt (Tall) x tt (Dwarf) -> 1 Tt (Tall) : 1 tt (Dwarf). If progeny show a 1:1 ratio, the unknown parent was heterozygous.

### 4. Incomplete Dominance Derivation
*Mirabilis jalapa* (Four o'clock plant) or *Antirrhinum majus* (Snapdragon) flower colour.
- **Parents:** Red (RR) x White (rr)
- **F1 Generation:** All Rr (Pink) -> *Blending of traits*
- **F2 Generation (Rr x Rr):**
  - **Punnett Square:** 1 RR (Red) : 2 Rr (Pink) : 1 rr (White)
- **Ratios:** Both Phenotypic and Genotypic ratios are identical (1:2:1).

### 5. Co-dominance: Inheritance of ABO Blood Groups
Controlled by gene *I*, which has three alleles: $I^A, I^B,$ and $i$.
- $I^A$ and $I^B$ produce slightly different forms of sugar on the RBC surface; $i$ does not produce any sugar.
- $I^A$ and $I^B$ are completely dominant over $i$, but co-dominant with each other.
- **If parent 1 gives $I^A$ and parent 2 gives $I^B$:** Offspring genotype is $I^A I^B$. Phenotype is Blood Group AB (both sugars present).

### 6. Morgan's Dihybrid Cross for Linkage (*Drosophila*)
Morgan crossed yellow-bodied, white-eyed females with brown-bodied, red-eyed males.
- **Cross A (Tightly linked genes):** Genes for body colour (y) and eye colour (w) are tightly linked.
  - Recombination frequency = 1.3%. Parental types = 98.7%.
- **Cross B (Loosely linked genes):** Genes for eye colour (w) and miniature wing (m) are loosely linked.
  - Recombination frequency = 37.2%. Parental types = 62.8%.

### 7. Haplodiploid Sex Determination in Honey Bees
- **Queen (2n = 32):** Undergoes meiosis to form haploid eggs (n = 16).
- **Drone (n = 16):** Produces sperms by mitosis (n = 16).
- **Fertilization:** Egg (16) + Sperm (16) -> Diploid female (32). *If fed royal jelly, becomes Queen; otherwise, Worker.*
- **Parthenogenesis:** Unfertilized Egg (16) -> Develops into Haploid male drone (16).
*Conclusion:* A drone has no father and cannot have sons, but has a grandfather and can have grandsons.

### 8. Molecular Mechanism of Sickle-cell Anemia
- **Type:** Autosomal recessive disease.
- **Mutation:** Point mutation (substitution).
- **Mechanism:** Substitution of Glutamic acid (Glu) by Valine (Val) at the sixth position of the beta-globin chain of the haemoglobin molecule.
- **Gene level:** The substitution occurs due to a single base substitution at the sixth codon of the beta-globin gene from **GAG to GUG**.
- **Physiological Effect:** The mutant haemoglobin molecule undergoes polymerization under low oxygen tension causing the change in the shape of the RBC from biconcave disc to an elongated sickle-like structure.
## PART 3: Diagram Blueprints & Labeling Checklists

### 1. Pedigree Analysis Symbols Blueprint
When drawing or interpreting a pedigree for the ISC exam, ensure you identify these symbols correctly:
- **Square:** Male
- **Circle:** Female
- **Diamond:** Sex unspecified
- **Filled/Shaded shape:** Affected individual
- **Unshaded shape:** Unaffected individual
- **Single horizontal line between square and circle:** Mating
- **Double horizontal line:** Consanguineous mating (mating between relatives)
- **Vertical line down from a mating line:** Connects to offspring
- **Siblings:** Horizontal line connecting vertical drop-downs, arranged left to right in order of birth.
- **Half-shaded or dotted:** Carrier (often for sex-linked recessive traits, though ISC generally uses full shading for affected and no shading for phenotypically normal carriers).

**Pedigree Checklist for ISC:**
1. Determine if the trait is dominant or recessive. (Does it skip generations? If yes, recessive. If affected children always have at least one affected parent, dominant).
2. Determine if it is autosomal or sex-linked. (Are males disproportionately affected? If yes, X-linked recessive. Is male-to-male transmission present? If yes, Autosomal).

### 2. Chromosomal Theory of Inheritance (Meiosis Diagram)
**Blueprint for Meiosis (Anaphase I and Anaphase II):**
- **G1 Phase:** Chromosomes are single chromatids.
- **G2 Phase:** Chromosomes are bivalent (two sister chromatids).
- **Meiosis I (Anaphase I):** Homologous chromosomes separate. *Crucial label:* "Independent assortment of homologous chromosomes." Ensure color coding (e.g., Orange/Green pair and Yellow/Red pair) shows random alignment at the metaphase plate.
- **Meiosis II (Anaphase II):** Sister chromatids separate.
- **Germ cells (Gametes):** Four haploid cells, each with one chromosome from each homologous pair. 

**Labeling Checklist:**
- [ ] Homologous Chromosomes
- [ ] Centromere
- [ ] Sister chromatids
- [ ] Pole of the cell
- [ ] Haploid gamete

### 3. Blueprint of Morgan’s Linkage Cross (*Drosophila*)
**Layout Structure:**
- **Parental Generation:** Show two X chromosomes for females, X and hooked Y for males.
- **Genes:** Use $y^+$ and $w^+$ for dominant (wild type) alleles, and $y$ and $w$ for recessive (mutant) alleles.
- **Cross A (Tight Linkage):** Show $y$ and $w$ placed very close together on the X chromosome.
- **Cross B (Loose Linkage):** Show $w$ and $m$ (miniature wing) placed far apart on the X chromosome.
- **F1 Generation:** Note the criss-cross inheritance (sons inherit maternal X, daughters inherit one maternal and one paternal X).
- **F2 Generation:** Clearly segregate the "Parental types" and "Recombinant types".

**Labeling Checklist:**
- [ ] Wild type ($+$ superscript)
- [ ] Mutant type
- [ ] Recombinant frequency percentage (1.3% for Cross A, 37.2% for Cross B)
- [ ] Parental type percentage

**Layout for Normal vs. Mutant Hb:**

2. **Mutant Hb (HbS):**
   - DNA Sequence: CAC (template strand) / GTG (coding strand)

### 5. Sex Determination Flowcharts Blueprint
**A. XX-XY Mechanism (Humans/Drosophila):**
- Parents: Female (44A + XX) x Male (44A + XY)
- Gametes: (22A + X) from Female ; (22A + X) & (22A + Y) from Male
- Offspring: 50% Female (44A + XX), 50% Male (44A + XY)

**B. ZZ-ZW Mechanism (Birds):**
- Parents: Female (AA + ZW) x Male (AA + ZZ)
- Gametes: (A + Z) & (A + W) from Female ; (A + Z) from Male
- Offspring: 50% Male (AA + ZZ), 50% Female (AA + ZW)

**C. XX-XO Mechanism (Grasshopper):**
- Parents: Female (AA + XX) x Male (AA + XO)
- Gametes: (A + X) from Female ; (A + X) & (A + O) from Male
- Offspring: 50% Female (AA + XX), 50% Male (AA + XO)
## PART 4: "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### 1. Distinction Tables (Crucial for ISC Subjective Questions)

**Table 1: Incomplete Dominance vs. Co-dominance**
| Feature | Incomplete Dominance | Co-dominance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Expression in F1** | F1 phenotype is an intermediate (blend) of the two parents. | F1 phenotype exhibits both parental traits equally. |
| **Dominance relationship** | Neither allele is completely dominant. | Both alleles are equally dominant (independent expression). |
| **Example** | Flower colour in Snapdragon (Pink F1). | ABO blood group in humans ($I^A I^B$ produces AB blood type). |

**Table 2: Pleiotropy vs. Polygenic Inheritance**
| Feature | Pleiotropy | Polygenic Inheritance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | A single gene controls multiple phenotypic traits. | Multiple genes (3 or more) control a single phenotypic trait. |
| **Nature of effect** | Qualitative effect; affects underlying metabolic pathways. | Quantitative effect; alleles have additive effects. |
| **Example** | Phenylketonuria, Sickle-cell anemia. | Human skin colour, human height. |

**Table 3: Linkage vs. Independent Assortment**
| Feature | Linkage | Independent Assortment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Location of genes** | Genes are located on the same chromosome (closely). | Genes are located on different non-homologous chromosomes. |
| **Inheritance pattern** | Genes tend to be inherited together. | Genes segregate independently of one another. |
| **Recombination** | Reduces recombination frequency (<50%). | Results in 50% recombinant types. |

**Table 4: Mendelian vs. Chromosomal Disorders**
| Feature | Mendelian Disorders | Chromosomal Disorders |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cause** | Alteration or mutation in a single gene. | Absence, excess, or abnormal arrangement of one or more whole chromosomes. |
| **Pedigree Analysis** | Can be traced in a family using a pedigree chart. | Cannot be easily traced via pedigree (often de novo nondisjunction). |
| **Examples** | Haemophilia, Cystic Fibrosis, Thalassemia. | Down's syndrome, Turner's syndrome, Klinefelter's syndrome. |

### 2. "Avoid the Trap" - Objective & Subjective Pitfalls

- **TRAP 1: Number of Gametes / Phenotypes**
  - *The Trap:* Guessing the number of gametes produced by a complex genotype (e.g., AaBbCc).
  - *The Escape:* Use the formula $2^n$, where $n$ is the number of **heterozygous** gene pairs. 
    - For AaBbCc: $n=3$, so $2^3 = 8$ types of gametes.
    - For AABbCc: $n=2$, so $2^2 = 4$ types of gametes.

- **TRAP 2: Haemophilia / Colour Blindness Crosses**
  - *The Trap:* Assuming a male can be a "carrier" for X-linked recessive traits.
  - *The Escape:* Males are **hemizygous** for X-linked traits ($X^cY$ or $X^hY$). They either have the disease or they don't. Only females can be carriers ($X^C X^c$). If a father has the disease, he passes the affected X chromosome to **all** his daughters (making them carriers if the mother is normal), but to **none** of his sons.

- **TRAP 3: Down's Syndrome Terminology**
  - *The Trap:* Writing "extra chromosome" without specifying.
  - *The Escape (ISC Standard):* You must write "**Trisomy of the 21st chromosome** due to non-disjunction during meiosis." Specifying 45 + XX or 45 + XY is highly recommended.

- **TRAP 4: Test Cross vs Back Cross**
  - *The Trap:* Confusing the two terms.
  - *The Escape:* A **Back cross** is crossing the F1 hybrid with *either* of the parents. A **Test cross** is a specific type of back cross where the F1 hybrid is crossed *only* with the **homozygous recessive** parent to determine genotype. All test crosses are back crosses, but not all back crosses are test crosses.

- **TRAP 5: Blood Group Progeny**
  - *The Trap:* Forgetting the $i$ allele when parents have Blood Group A and B.
  - *The Escape:* If a child has Blood Group O ($ii$), both parents MUST be heterozygous ($I^A i$ and $I^B i$). Always check for the hidden recessive allele in parental genotypes.
## PART 5: High-Yield Worked Model Problems

### Problem 1: X-Linked Recessive Cross (Colour Blindness)
**Question:** A normal visioned man whose father was colour-blind marries a woman whose father was also colour-blind. They have their first child as a daughter. What are the chances that this child would be colour-blind?

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. **Identify Parent Genotypes:** 
   - Man's vision is normal. His genotype is $X^C Y$. (His father's colour blindness does not affect his X chromosome, as he inherited Y from his father).
   - Woman's father was colour-blind ($X^c Y$), so he passed his $X^c$ to her. Since she is not stated to be colour-blind, she must be a carrier. Her genotype is $X^C X^c$.
2. **Form Gametes:**
   - Male gametes: $X^C$ and $Y$
   - Female gametes: $X^C$ and $X^c$
3. **Draw Punnett Square:**
| ♀ \ ♂ | $X^C$ | $Y$ |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **$X^C$** | $X^C X^C$ (Normal Daughter) | $X^C Y$ (Normal Son) |
| **$X^c$** | $X^C X^c$ (Carrier Daughter) | $X^c Y$ (Colour-blind Son) |
4. **Conclusion:** The question asks for the chance of the *daughter* being colour-blind. The daughters are either $X^C X^C$ or $X^C X^c$. None are colour-blind.
**Answer:** 0% chance.

**NEET Speed Method:**
Normal man ($XY$) x Carrier woman ($X^c X$). 
For a daughter to be colour-blind ($X^c X^c$), she MUST receive an $X^c$ from BOTH parents. The father is $XY$ and does not have an $X^c$ to give. Thus, 0% chance immediately.

### Problem 2: Blood Group Genetics
**Question:** A child has blood group O. If the father has blood group A and mother has blood group B, work out the genotypes of the parents and the possible genotypes of the other offspring.

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. **Child's Genotype:** Blood group O corresponds to genotype $ii$. The child must inherit one $i$ allele from each parent.
2. **Parent's Genotype:**
   - Father has blood group A, so he must be heterozygous: $I^A i$.
   - Mother has blood group B, so she must be heterozygous: $I^B i$.
3. **Cross:** $I^A i$ x $I^B i$
4. **Offspring Genotypes:**
   - $I^A I^B$ (Blood group AB)
   - $I^A i$ (Blood group A)
   - $I^B i$ (Blood group B)
   - $ii$ (Blood group O)
**Answer:** Parents are $I^A i$ and $I^B i$. Other possible offspring genotypes are $I^A I^B, I^A i,$ and $I^B i$.

### Problem 3: Dihybrid Cross Probabilities
**Question:** In a cross between AABB and aabb, what is the probability of obtaining an offspring with genotype AaBb in the F2 generation?

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. F1 generation is AaBb.
2. F2 generation is obtained by selfing F1: AaBb x AaBb.
3. Draw a 16-square Punnett square.
4. Count the number of AaBb genotypes. There are 4 out of 16.
**Answer:** 4/16 or 1/4.

**NEET Speed Method (Forked Line/Probability Method):**
Treat the dihybrid cross as two independent monohybrid crosses.
Cross 1: Aa x Aa -> Probability of getting Aa is 2/4 (or 1/2).
Cross 2: Bb x Bb -> Probability of getting Bb is 2/4 (or 1/2).
Combined probability = (Probability of Aa) x (Probability of Bb) = 1/2 x 1/2 = **1/4**.

### Problem 4: Pedigree Analysis
**Question:** Study the pedigree chart. Parents (unshaded circle and square) have three children: one affected daughter (shaded), one normal daughter, and one normal son. What is the mode of inheritance?

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. **Check for Dominance:** Unaffected parents have an affected offspring. The trait skips a generation. Therefore, it is a **Recessive** trait.
2. **Check for Sex-Linkage:** If it were X-linked recessive, the affected daughter ($X^a X^a$) must have inherited an affected $X^a$ from her father. This would mean the father MUST be affected ($X^a Y$). However, the father in the pedigree is unshaded (normal). Therefore, it cannot be X-linked recessive.
3. **Conclusion:** It must be **Autosomal Recessive**. Both parents are heterozygous carriers (Aa x Aa), producing an affected child (aa).

### Problem 5: Calculating Recombination Frequency
**Question:** In a test cross of a dihybrid *Drosophila* (heterozygous for genes A and B), the following progeny are obtained: Parental types = 800, Recombinant types = 200. Calculate the distance between genes A and B in map units.

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. **Total Progeny:** 800 + 200 = 1000.
2. **Formula:** Recombination Frequency = (Number of recombinants / Total progeny) x 100
3. **Calculation:** (200 / 1000) x 100 = 20%
4. **Map Units:** 1% recombination = 1 map unit (centiMorgan, cM).
**Answer:** The distance between genes A and B is **20 map units (20 cM)**.
