# Chapter 13: Organisms and Population

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology
- **Organism & Its Environment**: Ecology at the organismic level is essentially physiological ecology. Key abiotic factors: Temperature, Water, Light, Soil.
- **Responses to Abiotic Factors**:
  - **Regulators**: Maintain homeostasis (constant body temp & osmotic concentration). All birds and mammals.
  - **Conformers**: Body temperature/osmolarity changes with the ambient environment. (Majority of animals and nearly all plants).
  - **Migration**: Temporary movement to hospitable areas (e.g., Siberian cranes).
  - **Suspension**: Diapause (zooplankton), Hibernation (winter sleep - bears), Aestivation (summer sleep - snails/fish).
- **Adaptations**: Morphological, physiological, behavioral features. E.g., Kangaroo rat (internal fat oxidation for water), Allen's Rule (shorter ears/limbs in cold climates to minimize heat loss).
- **Populations**:
  - Attributes: Birth rate, Death rate, Sex ratio, Age distribution.
  - **Age Pyramids**: Expanding (Broad base, high pre-reproductive), Stable (Bell-shaped), Declining (Urn-shaped).
- **Population Interactions**:
  - **Mutualism (+/+)**: Both benefit. Lichens (fungus + algae), Mycorrhizae (fungi + plant roots), Fig and Fig wasp.
  - **Competition (-/-)**: Both harmed. Gause's Competitive Exclusion Principle. Macarthur's Resource Partitioning.
  - **Predation (+/-)**: Predator benefits, prey harmed. Conduits for energy transfer. Prey defenses (Camouflage, Monarch butterfly toxicity, Thorns).
  - **Parasitism (+/-)**: Ectoparasites (ticks/lice) vs Endoparasites (tapeworm). Brood parasitism (Cuckoo and Crow).
  - **Commensalism (+/0)**: One benefits, other neutral. E.g., Barnacles on whale, Cattle egret and grazing cattle, Sea anemone and clownfish.
  - **Amensalism (-/0)**: One harmed, other neutral. E.g., Penicillium mold killing bacteria.

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms
**Population Growth Models:**
1. **Exponential Growth**: When resources are unlimited. Equation: $dN/dt = rN$ (where r = intrinsic rate of natural increase). Results in a **J-shaped curve**. $N_t = N_0 e^{rt}$.
2. **Logistic Growth**: When resources are limited. Verhulst-Pearl Logistic Growth equation: $dN/dt = rN \left( \frac{K - N}{K} \right)$ (where K = Carrying capacity). Results in a **Sigmoid (S-shaped) curve**. Realistic model.

## 3. Diagram Blueprints & Labeling Checklists
- **Population Growth Curves**: Y-axis (Population density N), X-axis (Time t). Label the J-curve ($dN/dt = rN$) and S-curve ($dN/dt = rN[(K-N)/K]$). Draw the horizontal asymptote 'K' for the S-curve.
- **Age Pyramids**: Label the three horizontal tiers: Pre-reproductive, Reproductive, and Post-reproductive.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

|-------------|-----------|-----------|---------|
| Mutualism | + | + | Fig & Wasp, Lichen |
| Commensalism | + | 0 | Epiphyte on Mango tree |
| Amensalism | - | 0 | Antibiotic mold vs bacteria |

**Trap Alert (Objective):** In competition, both species suffer (-/-) because they expend energy fighting for the same limited resource, even if one "wins".
**Trap Alert (Subjective):** **Gause's Principle** applies only when resources are *limiting*. If resources are abundant, species can co-exist.

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems
**Q: A population of paramecium in a pond has 50 individuals. Over a year, 20 are born and 10 die. Calculate the intrinsic rate of natural increase (r) and the new population size.**
- **ISC Step-by-Step**:
  - Initial Population ($N_0$) = 50.
  - Birth rate ($b$) = Births / Initial Pop = 20 / 50 = 0.4 per year.
  - Death rate ($d$) = Deaths / Initial Pop = 10 / 50 = 0.2 per year.
  - Intrinsic rate of increase ($r$) = $b - d$ = 0.4 - 0.2 = 0.2 per year.
  - Net increase in population = Births - Deaths = 20 - 10 = 10.
  - New population size = $N_0$ + Net increase = 50 + 10 = 60.
- **NEET Speed Method**: $r = b-d = (20-10)/50 = 0.2$. New size = $50 + 10 = 60$.
