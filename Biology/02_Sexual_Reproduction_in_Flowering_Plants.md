# Chapter 02: Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

### Pre-fertilization: Structures and Events
**Stamen, Microsporangium and Pollen Grain**
* **Stamen**: The male reproductive organ consisting of a **filament** and an **anther**. Anthers are generally **bilobed**, with each lobe having two thecae (**dithecous**).
* **Microsporangium**: Four microsporangia are located at the corners. The microsporangial wall consists of four layers: **epidermis, endothecium, middle layers, and tapetum**. The **tapetum** nourishes the developing pollen grains.
* **Microsporogenesis**: The process of formation of microspores from a **pollen mother cell (PMC)** through meiosis. The microspores are arranged in a **microspore tetrad**.
* **Pollen Grain**: Represents the **male gametophyte**. Has a two-layered wall: outer **exine** (made of highly resistant **sporopollenin**, absent at **germ pores**) and inner **intine** (pecto-cellulosic). A mature pollen grain contains a larger **vegetative cell** and a smaller **generative cell** (which divides into two **male gametes**). Shed at 2-celled (60% angiosperms) or 3-celled stage.

**Pistil, Megasporangium (ovule) and Embryo sac**
* **Pistil**: Consists of **stigma, style, and ovary**. May be **syncarpous** (fused carpels) or **apocarpous** (free carpels).
* **Megasporangium (Ovule)**: Attached to the placenta by a **funicle**. The body fuses with the funicle at the **hilum**. Has protective **integuments** enclosing the **nucellus**, with a small opening called the **micropyle**. The basal part is the **chalaza**.
* **Megasporogenesis**: Formation of megaspores from the **megaspore mother cell (MMC)** in the micropylar region. Usually, one megaspore is functional, and three degenerate (**monosporic development**).
* **Embryo Sac (Female Gametophyte)**: 8-nucleate, 7-celled structure. Contains a 3-celled **egg apparatus** (one egg cell + two **synergids** with **filiform apparatus**) at the micropylar end, 3 **antipodals** at the chalazal end, and a large **central cell** with 2 **polar nuclei**.

### Pollination
Transfer of pollen grains to the stigma.
* **Autogamy**: Within the same flower (**chasmogamous** vs **cleistogamous**).
* **Geitonogamy**: Between flowers of the same plant (functionally cross, genetically self).
* **Xenogamy**: Between different plants (genetically cross).
* **Agents**: Abiotic (wind, water) and Biotic (insects, birds, bats). Water pollination in *Vallisneria, Hydrilla, Zostera*.
* **Outbreeding Devices**: Mechanisms to prevent self-pollination (dichogamy, self-incompatibility, dioecy).
* **Pollen-Pistil Interaction**: A dynamic process involving pollen recognition followed by promotion or inhibition of pollen tube growth.

### Double Fertilization
* **Syngamy**: Fusion of one male gamete with the egg cell, forming a diploid **zygote**.
* **Triple Fusion**: Fusion of the second male gamete with the two polar nuclei to form a triploid **Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN)**.
* **Double Fertilization**: The occurrence of both syngamy and triple fusion, unique to angiosperms.

### Post-fertilization: Structures and Events
* **Endosperm**: Develops from the PEN (usually precedes embryo development). Nourishes the developing embryo. (e.g., coconut water is free-nuclear endosperm, white kernel is cellular endosperm). Seeds can be **albuminous** (retain endosperm, e.g., wheat, maize) or **non-albuminous** (consume endosperm, e.g., pea, groundnut).
* **Embryo**: Develops from the zygote at the micropylar end. Consists of an **embryonal axis** and one (monocots) or two (dicots) **cotyledons**. Includes **plumule, radicle, hypocotyl, and epicotyl**.
* **Seed**: Matured ovule. Integuments become seed coats. **Perisperm** is the residual, persistent nucellus (e.g., black pepper, beet).
* **Fruit**: Matured ovary. **True fruits** develop only from the ovary. **False fruits** involve other floral parts like the thalamus (e.g., apple, strawberry). **Parthenocarpic fruits** develop without fertilization (e.g., banana).

### Apomixis and Polyembryony
* **Apomixis**: A form of asexual reproduction that mimics sexual reproduction; seed formation without fertilization.
* **Polyembryony**: Occurrence of more than one embryo in a seed (e.g., citrus, mango).

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### Mechanism of Double Fertilization
1. **Pollen Germination**: Compatible pollen grain absorbs water and nutrients on the stigma. The intine protrudes through a germ pore to form a **pollen tube**.
2. **Tube Growth**: The tube grows through the style tissues, directed by chemotropic signals. The generative cell divides into two male gametes if shed at the 2-celled stage.
3. **Entry into Ovule**: The tube enters the ovule, usually through the micropyle (**porogamy**).
4. **Entry into Embryo Sac**: Guided by the **filiform apparatus** of the synergids, the tube enters one degenerating synergid and releases the two male gametes into the synergid cytoplasm.
5. **Syngamy and Triple Fusion**: Gamete 1 fuses with the egg nucleus. Gamete 2 moves to the central cell and fuses with the polar nuclei.

## 3. Diagram Blueprints & Labeling Checklists

1. **T.S. of a Microsporangium**
   * *Blueprint*: Show the four wall layers. Focus on the tapetum cells (multinucleate/dense cytoplasm) directly surrounding the sporogenous tissue.
   * *Checklist*: Epidermis, Endothecium, Middle layers, Tapetum, Sporogenous tissue.
2. **Anatropous Ovule**
   * *Blueprint*: Inverted ovule with micropyle close to the funicle. Show the embryo sac embedded within the nucellus.
   * *Checklist*: Funicle, Hilum, Outer Integument, Inner Integument, Micropyle, Micropylar pole, Chalazal pole, Nucellus, Embryo sac.
3. **Mature Embryo Sac (7-celled, 8-nucleate)**
   * *Blueprint*: Oval structure. Group of 3 cells at top (chalazal), 3 at bottom (micropylar).
   * *Checklist*: Antipodals, Central cell, Polar nuclei, Egg cell, Synergids, Filiform apparatus.
4. **Dicot Embryo vs. Monocot Embryo (L.S. of Grass Embryo)**
   * *Checklist (Dicot)*: Plumule, Cotyledons, Hypocotyl, Radicle, Root cap.
   * *Checklist (Monocot)*: Scutellum, Coleoptile, Shoot apex, Epiblast, Radicle, Root cap, Coleorhiza.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Trap Warnings (ISC & NEET)
* **Ploidy Traps**: Remember ploidy levels: Nucellus (2n), Tapetum (2n, but can be polyploid), Endosperm (3n in angiosperms, but n in gymnosperms), Synergid (n), Antipodal (n), Zygote (2n), PEN (3n).
* **Parthenocarpy vs. Apomixis**: Parthenocarpy produces *fruit* without fertilization (seedless). Apomixis produces *seeds* without fertilization.
* **Geitonogamy**: Functionally cross-pollination (requires pollinator) but genetically self-pollination (same plant).

### Distinction Table

| Feature | Autogamy | Geitonogamy | Xenogamy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pollen Source** | Same flower | Different flower, same plant | Different plant |
| **Genetic Result** | Clonal / Homozygous | Clonal / Homozygous | Recombinant / Heterozygous |
| **Requirement** | Cleistogamy / synchronization | Requires agents | Requires agents |

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

**Problem 1 (Objective/NEET):** If the diploid number of chromosomes in an angiospermic plant is 24, what will be the number of chromosomes in its nucellus, synergids, and endosperm?
**Speed Method:**
1. 2n = 24 $\rightarrow$ n = 12.
2. Nucellus is 2n $\rightarrow$ 24.
3. Synergids are n $\rightarrow$ 12.
4. Endosperm is 3n $\rightarrow$ 36.
*Answer:* 24, 12, 36.

**Problem 2 (Subjective/ISC):** Explain the structure of a mature angiospermic embryo sac. (3 marks)
**Step-by-Step Method:**
1. **Introduction**: A mature embryo sac is 7-celled and 8-nucleate.
2. **Micropylar End**: Contains the egg apparatus, consisting of two synergids and one egg cell. Synergids possess a filiform apparatus that guides the pollen tube.
3. **Chalazal End**: Contains three antipodal cells which degenerate after fertilization.
4. **Center**: A large central cell containing two polar nuclei, which eventually fuse to form a secondary nucleus prior to triple fusion.
