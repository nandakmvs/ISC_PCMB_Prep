# 02. Sexual Reproduction in Flowering Plants

## PART 1: EXHAUSTIVE THEORY & PRECISE ISC TERMINOLOGY

### 1. Pre-Fertilization: Structures and Events

#### A. Stamen, Microsporangium, and Pollen Grain
*   **Stamen**: The male reproductive unit, consisting of a long, slender stalk called the **filament** and a terminal, generally **bilobed anther**.
*   **Anther Structure**: A typical angiosperm anther is **bilobed**, with each lobe having two thecae, meaning they are **dithecous**. A longitudinal groove runs lengthwise separating the thecae. The anther is a four-sided (**tetragonal**) structure consisting of four **microsporangia** located at the corners, two in each lobe. The microsporangia develop further and become **pollen sacs**.
*   **Structure of Microsporangium**: In a transverse section, a typical microsporangium appears near circular in outline. It is generally surrounded by four wall layers:
    1.  **Epidermis**: The outermost, single-layered protective covering.
    2.  **Endothecium**: Inner to the epidermis, cells possess fibrous thickenings (of alpha-cellulose) which help in the **dehiscence of the anther** to release pollen.
    3.  **Middle Layers**: 1-3 layers of ephemeral cells that degenerate at maturity.
    4.  **Tapetum**: The innermost wall layer. It **nourishes the developing pollen grains**. Cells of the tapetum possess dense cytoplasm and generally have **more than one nucleus** (multinucleate/polyploid) due to endomitosis.
*   **Sporogenous Tissue**: When the anther is young, a group of compactly arranged homogenous cells called the **sporogenous tissue** occupies the centre of each microsporangium.
*   **Microsporogenesis**: The process of formation of microspores from a **Pollen Mother Cell (PMC)** through meiosis. The microspores are arranged in a cluster of four cells called a **microspore tetrad**.
*   **Pollen Grain (Male Gametophyte)**: Spherical structures measuring about 25-50 micrometers in diameter.
    *   **Exine**: The hard outer layer made up of **sporopollenin**, which is one of the most resistant organic materials known (can withstand high temperatures, strong acids, and alkalis; no enzyme that degrades sporopollenin is so far known). It has prominent apertures called **germ pores** where sporopollenin is absent.
    *   **Intine**: The inner wall, which is a thin and continuous layer made up of **cellulose and pectin**.
    *   **Vegetative Cell**: Bigger, has abundant food reserve and a large irregularly shaped nucleus.
    *   **Generative Cell**: Small, floats in the cytoplasm of the vegetative cell. It is spindle-shaped with dense cytoplasm and a nucleus.
    *   *Note*: In over 60% of angiosperms, pollen grains are shed at this **2-celled stage**. In the remaining species, the generative cell divides mitotically to give rise to two male gametes before pollen grains are shed (**3-celled stage**).

#### B. The Pistil, Megasporangium (Ovule), and Embryo Sac
*   **Gynoecium**: Represents the female reproductive part of the flower. It may consist of a single pistil (**monocarpellary**) or more than one pistil (**multicarpellary**). When there are more than one, the pistils may be fused together (**syncarpous**) or may be free (**apocarpous**).
*   **Pistil Components**:
    1.  **Stigma**: The landing platform for pollen grains.
    2.  **Style**: The elongated slender part beneath the stigma.
    3.  **Ovary**: The basal bulged part of the pistil. Inside the ovary is the **ovarian cavity (locule)**. The **placenta** is located inside the ovarian cavity.
*   **Megasporangium (Ovule)**: Arises from the placenta.
    *   **Funicle**: The stalk by which the ovule is attached to the placenta.
    *   **Hilum**: The point of attachment of the body of the ovule with the funicle. (The junction between ovule and funicle).
    *   **Integuments**: One or two protective envelopes surrounding the ovule.
    *   **Micropyle**: A small opening at the tip where the integuments do not cover the nucellus.
    *   **Chalaza**: The basal part of the ovule opposite the micropylar end.
    *   **Nucellus**: A mass of cells enclosed within the integuments, having abundant reserve food materials.
*   **Megasporogenesis**: The process of formation of megaspores from the **Megaspore Mother Cell (MMC)**. The MMC is a large cell containing dense cytoplasm and a prominent nucleus. The MMC undergoes meiotic division to form four megaspores.
*   **Female Gametophyte (Embryo Sac)**: In a majority of flowering plants, one of the megaspores is functional while the other three degenerate. The functional megaspore develops into the female gametophyte (**monosporic development**).
*   **Mature Embryo Sac (7-celled, 8-nucleate structure)**:
    *   **Egg Apparatus**: Located at the micropylar end, consists of two **synergids** and one **egg cell**. Synergids have special cellular thickenings at the micropylar tip called the **filiform apparatus**, which plays an important role in guiding the pollen tubes into the synergid.
    *   **Antipodal Cells**: Three cells located at the chalazal end.
    *   **Central Cell**: The largest cell, containing two **polar nuclei**.

### 2. Pollination
The transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a pistil.
*   **Autogamy**: Transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of the *same flower*. Requires synchrony in pollen release and stigma receptivity.
    *   **Chasmogamous flowers**: Open flowers with exposed anthers and stigma.
    *   **Cleistogamous flowers**: Flowers that do not open at all. Autogamy is obligatory; ensures seed set even in the absence of pollinators (e.g., *Viola, Oxalis, Commelina*).
*   **Geitonogamy**: Transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of *another flower of the same plant*. Functionally cross-pollination (requires agent), but genetically similar to autogamy.
*   **Xenogamy**: Transfer of pollen grains from the anther to the stigma of a *different plant*. This is the only type of pollination that brings **genetically different** types of pollen grains to the stigma.

#### Agents of Pollination
*   **Anemophily (Wind)**: Requires light and non-sticky pollen, well-exposed stamens, and large, often feathery stigma. Often have a single ovule in each ovary and numerous flowers packed into an inflorescence (e.g., grasses, corn cob).
*   **Hydrophily (Water)**: Quite rare in flowering plants (limited to ~30 genera, mostly monocots like *Vallisneria, Hydrilla, Zostera*). Pollen grains are often long, ribbon-like, and protected from wetting by a mucilaginous covering.
*   **Zoophily (Animals)**: Entomophily (Insects) is the most common. Flowers are large, colourful, fragrant, and rich in nectar. Small flowers cluster into an inflorescence. Pollen is sticky. Animal rewards: Nectar, pollen, and safe places to lay eggs (e.g., *Amorphophallus*, Yucca-moth relationship).

#### Outbreeding Devices
Devices to discourage self-pollination and encourage cross-pollination to avoid **inbreeding depression**:
1.  **Dichogamy**: Non-synchronization of pollen release and stigma receptivity (Protandry vs Protogyny).
2.  **Herkogamy**: Physical barrier between anther and stigma.
3.  **Self-incompatibility**: A genetic mechanism preventing self-pollen from fertilizing the ovules by inhibiting pollen germination or pollen tube growth.
4.  **Dicliny (Unisexuality)**: Production of unisexual flowers (monoecious plants like castor and maize prevent autogamy but not geitonogamy; dioecious plants like papaya prevent both).

#### Pollen-Pistil Interaction
The pistil has the ability to recognise the pollen, whether it is of the right type (**compatible**) or of the wrong type (**incompatible**). This dialogue is mediated by chemical components of the pollen interacting with those of the pistil. Following compatible pollination, the pollen grain germinates on the stigma to produce a **pollen tube** through one of the germ pores.

### 3. Double Fertilization
A unique event in angiosperms.
1.  **Syngamy**: One of the male gametes (n) fuses with the egg cell (n) to form a **zygote (2n)**.
2.  **Triple Fusion**: The other male gamete (n) fuses with the two polar nuclei (n+n) in the central cell to produce a **Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN, 3n)**.

### 4. Post-Fertilization Events
*   **Endosperm**: Develops from the PEN. Endosperm development precedes embryo development to ensure assured nutrition to the developing embryo. (Types: Nuclear, Cellular, Helobial).
*   **Embryo**: Develops at the micropylar end of the embryo sac where the zygote is situated.
    *   Stages: Proembryo $\rightarrow$ Globular $\rightarrow$ Heart-shaped $\rightarrow$ Mature embryo.
    *   **Dicot Embryo**: Has an embryonal axis and two **cotyledons**. The portion above the level of cotyledons is the **epicotyl** (terminates with the **plumule**). The cylindrical portion below the level of cotyledons is the **hypocotyl** (terminates at the **radicle** or root tip).
    *   **Monocot Embryo**: Has only one cotyledon called **scutellum** (situated towards one side of the axis). The lower end of the axis has the radicle and root cap enclosed in an undifferentiated sheath called the **coleorhiza**. The epicotyl has a shoot apex and a few leaf primordia enclosed in a hollow foliar structure, the **coleoptile**.
*   **Seed**: Final product of sexual reproduction. Consists of seed coat(s), cotyledon(s), and an embryo axis.
    *   **Non-albuminous (Exalbuminous)**: No residual endosperm (e.g., pea, groundnut).
    *   **Albuminous**: Retain a part of endosperm (e.g., wheat, maize, barley, castor).
    *   **Perisperm**: Residual, persistent nucellus (e.g., black pepper, beet).
*   **Fruit**: Ovary develops into a fruit.
    *   **True Fruit**: Develops solely from the ovary.
    *   **False Fruit**: Thalamus also contributes to fruit formation (e.g., apple, strawberry, cashew).
    *   **Parthenocarpic Fruit**: Develops without fertilization (e.g., banana). Seedless and can be induced by growth hormones.

### 5. Apomixis and Polyembryony
*   **Apomixis**: A form of asexual reproduction that mimics sexual reproduction; produces seeds without fertilization (e.g., Asteraceae, grasses). Can occur if a diploid egg cell is formed without meiosis and develops into an embryo, or if nucellar cells surrounding the embryo sac protrude into it and develop into embryos.
*   **Polyembryony**: Occurrence of more than one embryo in a seed (e.g., *Citrus*, mango).

## PART 2: STEP-BY-STEP DERIVATIONS & MECHANISMS

### Mechanism 1: Microsporogenesis & Development of Male Gametophyte
**Goal**: From Sporogenous Tissue to a 3-Celled Pollen Grain ready for fertilization.
**Step 1: MMC Formation**
Cells of the sporogenous tissue inside the microsporangium act as **Pollen Mother Cells (PMCs)** or Microspore Mother Cells (2n).
**Step 2: Meiosis & Tetrad Formation**
Each PMC undergoes meiosis (Meiosis I & II) to form four haploid **microspores (n)**. These are arranged in a **microspore tetrad**.
**Step 3: Dissociation**
As the anthers mature and dehydrate, the microspores dissociate from each other and develop into **pollen grains**.
**Step 4: Pollen Grain Mitosis (1st Division)**
Inside the pollen grain, the haploid nucleus undergoes mitosis (unequal division). It forms a large **Vegetative Cell** (abundant food, large irregular nucleus) and a small **Generative Cell** (spindle-shaped, dense cytoplasm) floating in the vegetative cell's cytoplasm. *This is the 2-celled stage at which ~60% of angiosperms shed pollen.*
**Step 5: Pollen Grain Mitosis (2nd Division - Post-pollination or before shedding)**
The generative cell divides mitotically to give rise to **two male gametes**. *This forms the 3-celled stage.*

### Mechanism 2: Megasporogenesis & Development of Female Gametophyte (Monosporic Development)
**Goal**: From MMC to a 7-celled, 8-nucleate Embryo Sac.
**Step 1: Differentiation of MMC**
A single **Megaspore Mother Cell (MMC, 2n)** differentiates in the micropylar region of the nucellus in the ovule.
**Step 2: Meiosis**
The MMC undergoes meiosis to form four haploid **megaspores (n)** arranged in a linear tetrad.
**Step 3: Degeneration**
In a majority of flowering plants, the three megaspores at the micropylar end degenerate, leaving one **functional megaspore** at the chalazal end.
**Step 4: Free Nuclear Mitosis (1st, 2nd, and 3rd)**
*   The nucleus of the functional megaspore divides mitotically to form **two nuclei**, which move to opposite poles (2-nucleate embryo sac).
*   Two more sequential mitotic nuclear divisions result in the **4-nucleate** and later the **8-nucleate** stages of the embryo sac.
*   *Strictly free nuclear*: Divisions are not followed immediately by cell wall formation.
**Step 5: Cellular Organization (Cell Wall Formation)**
*   **Micropylar End**: 3 nuclei are surrounded by cell walls to form the **Egg Apparatus** (1 egg cell + 2 synergids).
*   **Chalazal End**: 3 nuclei are surrounded by cell walls to form the **Antipodal cells**.
*   **Center**: The remaining 2 nuclei (polar nuclei) are situated in the large **Central Cell**.
**Result**: A mature embryo sac with **8 nuclei and 7 cells**.

### Mechanism 3: Pollen-Pistil Interaction to Double Fertilization
**Step 1: Recognition and Germination**
Compatible pollen grain lands on the stigma, absorbs moisture and nutrients, and germinates to produce a **pollen tube** through a germ pore.
**Step 2: Tube Growth**
The pollen tube grows through the tissues of the stigma and style via chemotropic movement and reaches the ovary. The vegetative nucleus leads, followed by the two male gametes.
**Step 3: Entry into the Ovule**
The pollen tube enters the ovule through the **micropyle**.
**Step 4: Entry into the Synergid**
The pollen tube enters one of the **synergids** through the **filiform apparatus** (which guides the entry).
**Step 5: Gamete Release and Double Fertilization**
The pollen tube releases the two male gametes into the cytoplasm of the synergid.
*   **Syngamy**: Male gamete 1 (n) + Egg cell (n) $\rightarrow$ **Zygote (2n)**.
*   **Triple Fusion**: Male gamete 2 (n) + Two polar nuclei (n+n) $\rightarrow$ **Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN) (3n)**.

## PART 4: "AVOID THE TRAP" & DISTINCTION TABLES

### 1. Objective Traps (NEET/JEE & ISC MCQs)

*   **Trap 1: The ploidy levels.**
    *   *Nucellus*: 2n
    *   *MMC / PMC*: 2n
    *   *Microspore / Megaspore*: n
    *   *Tapetum*: Generally polyploid (often considered 2n initially but becomes multinucleate).
    *   *Endosperm in Angiosperms*: 3n (forms *after* fertilization).
    *   *Endosperm in Gymnosperms*: n (forms *before* fertilization). **HIGH YIELD NEET TRAP.**
    *   *Synergids, Antipodals, Egg*: n
    *   *Integuments*: 2n
*   **Trap 2: Exine vs. Intine Composition.**
    *   Exine is **Sporopollenin** (resistant).
    *   Intine is **Cellulose & Pectin** (grows into the pollen tube).
*   **Trap 3: Cleistogamy vs Chasmogamy.**
    *   Cleistogamous flowers ensure seed set *even in the absence of pollinators*.
*   **Trap 4: Geitonogamy's dual nature.**
    *   *Ecologically/Functionally*: Cross-pollination (requires agent).
    *   *Genetically*: Self-pollination (Autogamy) since pollen comes from the same plant.
*   **Trap 5: Albuminous vs Non-albuminous seeds.**
    *   *Albuminous*: Monocots (wheat, maize) + **Castor** (Dicot exception!).
    *   *Non-albuminous*: Dicots (pea, bean, groundnut) + **Orchids** (Monocot exception!).
*   **Trap 6: Perisperm vs Endosperm.**
    *   *Perisperm* is the remnant of the **Nucellus (2n)** (e.g., Black pepper, beet).
    *   *Endosperm* is the product of triple fusion **(3n)**.

### 2. Subjective Traps (ISC Specific Rules)
*   **Avoid the Trap in definitions:** When defining *Double Fertilization*, you MUST mention both *Syngamy* and *Triple Fusion*. Missing either results in a 0.
*   **Avoid the Trap in drawing the Embryo Sac:** Always label the *Micropylar* and *Chalazal* ends. Synergids are strictly at the micropylar end. If you flip the drawing without labeling the poles, marks are deducted.

## PART 5: HIGH-YIELD WORKED MODEL PROBLEMS

### 1. ISC Step-by-Step Subjective Problems

**Q1: Explain the development of a mature male gametophyte in angiosperms. (3 Marks)**
**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1.  **Initial Stage:** The microspore (pollen grain) is the first cell of the male gametophyte. It possesses a dense cytoplasm and a centrally located haploid nucleus.
2.  **First Mitotic Division:** The haploid nucleus undergoes an unequal mitotic division. This results in the formation of two unequal cells: a larger vegetative cell (with abundant food reserve and a large irregular nucleus) and a smaller, spindle-shaped generative cell which floats in the cytoplasm of the vegetative cell. This is the 2-celled stage.
3.  **Second Mitotic Division:** The generative cell undergoes a second mitotic division to form two non-motile male gametes. This can occur either before shedding (shed at 3-celled stage) or in the pollen tube after pollination. The mature male gametophyte is 3-celled.

**Q2: Describe the structure of a typical embryo sac. Why is it called 7-celled and 8-nucleate? (5 Marks)**
**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1.  **Introduction:** The typical female gametophyte of angiosperms is the embryo sac, which develops from a single functional megaspore (monosporic development).
2.  **Micropylar End:** Three cells are grouped together to form the **egg apparatus**. This consists of two synergids and one egg cell. Synergids have special cellular thickenings called the filiform apparatus. (Total: 3 cells, 3 nuclei).
3.  **Chalazal End:** Three cells are situated at the chalazal end, called the **antipodals**. (Total: 3 cells, 3 nuclei).
4.  **Central Cell:** The remaining large cell in the centre is the **central cell**. It contains two nuclei called **polar nuclei**. (Total: 1 cell, 2 nuclei).
5.  **Conclusion:** Summing up the cells: 3 (egg apparatus) + 3 (antipodals) + 1 (central cell) = 7 cells. Summing up the nuclei: 3 + 3 + 2 = 8 nuclei. Hence, a mature embryo sac is 7-celled and 8-nucleate.

**Q3: What is Double Fertilization? Explain its significance. (3 Marks)**
**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1.  **Definition:** Double fertilization is a unique phenomenon in angiosperms involving two separate fusion events in the embryo sac: Syngamy and Triple Fusion.
2.  **Syngamy:** One male gamete (n) fuses with the egg cell (n) to form a diploid zygote (2n), which develops into an embryo.
3.  **Triple Fusion:** The second male gamete (n) fuses with the two polar nuclei (n+n) in the central cell to form a triploid Primary Endosperm Nucleus (PEN, 3n), which develops into the endosperm.
4.  **Significance:** It ensures that the nutritive tissue (endosperm) develops only if fertilization of the egg has occurred, saving the plant's energy.

### 2. NEET/JEE Speed Problems & Shortcuts

**Q4 (NEET Speed Method): How many meiotic divisions are required to produce 100 seeds in a typical angiosperm?**
*   **Concept:** 1 seed requires 1 functional male gamete (from 1 pollen grain) and 1 functional egg cell (from 1 embryo sac).
*   **Male side:** 1 PMC (meiosis) $\rightarrow$ 4 pollen grains. To get 100 pollen grains, you need $100 / 4 = 25$ meiotic divisions.
*   **Female side:** 1 MMC (meiosis) $\rightarrow$ 4 megaspores $\rightarrow$ 1 functional megaspore (embryo sac). To get 100 embryo sacs, you need 100 meiotic divisions.
*   **Total Meiosis:** $25 + 100 = 125$.
*   **NEET Shortcut:** Total meiosis for 'n' seeds in typical angiosperm = $n + \frac{n}{4} = \frac{5n}{4}$. For $n=100$, $\frac{5(100)}{4} = 125$.

**Q5 (NEET Speed Method): If the leaf cell of a typical angiosperm has 24 chromosomes, what will be the number of chromosomes in its nucellus, tapetum, endosperm, and synergid respectively?**
*   **Concept:** Identify the ploidy of the given cell and calculate 'n'. Leaf cell = Somatic cell = 2n = 24. Therefore, n = 12.
*   **Nucellus:** 2n = 24.
*   **Tapetum:** Polyploid, but generally considered starting as 2n (or higher), but often the question implies the base somatic level or polyploid level. If strictly asked, it's multinucleate/polyploid, but basic chromosomal setup before endomitosis is 2n = 24. (Look at the options).
*   **Endosperm:** 3n = $3 \times 12 = 36$.
*   **Synergid:** n = 12.
*   **Answer:** 24, 24 (or >24), 36, 12.

**Q6 (NEET Trap): Which of the following is not an outbreeding device?**
(A) Dichogamy
(B) Herkogamy
(C) Cleistogamy
(D) Self-incompatibility
*   **Speed Logic:** Outbreeding devices *prevent* self-pollination. Cleistogamous flowers never open, ensuring *obligate self-pollination* (inbreeding).
*   **Answer:** (C)
