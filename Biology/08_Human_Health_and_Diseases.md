# Chapter 08: Human Health and Diseases

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

### Common Diseases in Humans
* **Health**: A state of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease.
* **Pathogens**: Disease-causing organisms.
* **Bacterial Diseases**:
  * **Typhoid**: *Salmonella typhi*. Contaminated food/water. Affects small intestine. Symptoms: Sustained high fever (39-40°C), weakness, stomach pain, constipation. Confirmed by **Widal test**. (Classic case: Typhoid Mary).
  * **Pneumonia**: *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Haemophilus influenzae*. Infects alveoli of lungs (fluid-filled). Spread by inhaling droplets. Symptoms: Fever, chills, cough, headache. Severe cases: lips/nails turn grey-bluish.
* **Viral Diseases**:
  * **Common Cold**: Rhino viruses. Infects upper respiratory tract (nose, not lungs). Spread by droplets/contaminated objects. Symptoms: Nasal congestion, sore throat, cough (lasts 3-7 days).
* **Protozoan Diseases**:
  * **Malaria**: *Plasmodium* species (*P. vivax, P. malariae, P. falciparum* - most fatal). Vector: Female *Anopheles* mosquito. Sporozoites (infective stage) $\rightarrow$ Liver (multiplication) $\rightarrow$ RBCs (rupture causes chill/fever due to release of toxic **hemozoin**).
  * **Amoebiasis (Amoebic dysentery)**: *Entamoeba histolytica*. Vector: Houseflies. Affects large intestine. Symptoms: Constipation, abdominal pain, stools with excess mucus and blood clots.
* **Helminthic Diseases**:
  * **Ascariasis**: *Ascaris* (roundworm). Intestinal parasite. Spread by contaminated soil, water, plants. Symptoms: Internal bleeding, muscular pain, blockage of intestinal passage.
  * **Filariasis (Elephantiasis)**: *Wuchereria* (*W. bancrofti, W. malayi*). Vector: Female *Culex* mosquito. Chronic inflammation of lymphatic vessels of lower limbs.
* **Fungal Diseases**:
  * **Ringworm**: *Microsporum, Trichophyton, Epidermophyton*. Spread by soil or using towels/clothes of infected individuals. Symptoms: Dry, scaly lesions with intense itching on skin, nails, scalp. Thrives in moisture (skin folds, groin).

### Immunity
* The overall ability of the host to fight the disease-causing organisms conferred by the immune system.
* **Innate Immunity**: Non-specific, present at birth. Consists of 4 barriers:
  1. **Physical barriers**: Skin, mucus coating of respiratory/GI tracts.
  2. **Physiological barriers**: Acid in stomach, saliva in mouth, tears in eyes.
  3. **Cellular barriers**: Polymorphonuclear leukocytes (PMNL-neutrophils), monocytes, natural killer (NK) cells, macrophages in tissues.
  4. **Cytokine barriers**: Virus-infected cells secrete proteins called **interferons** which protect non-infected cells from further viral infection.
* **Acquired Immunity**: Pathogen-specific, characterized by memory. Primary response (low intensity) followed by secondary/anamnestic response (high intensity).
  * Mediated by B-lymphocytes (produce antibodies - **Humoral immune response**) and T-lymphocytes (help B cells and attack directly - **Cell-mediated immune response**).
  * **Antibody Structure**: 4 polypeptide chains ($H_2L_2$). IgA, IgM, IgE, IgG.
* **Active vs Passive Immunity**:
  * **Active**: Host produces antibodies upon exposure to antigens (slow). E.g., natural infection or vaccination.
  * **Passive**: Readymade antibodies are directly given (fast). E.g., Colostrum (IgA), maternal antibodies across placenta (IgG), Anti-tetanus serum, Anti-venom.
* **Vaccination and Immunisation**: Based on the property of 'memory'. Vaccine (weakened pathogen or antigenic protein) generates primary response and memory B/T cells. Recombinant DNA technology allows large-scale production of vaccines (e.g., Hepatitis B vaccine from yeast).
* **Allergies**: Exaggerated immune response to certain antigens (**allergens**). Due to release of chemicals like histamine and serotonin from mast cells. Involves **IgE** antibodies. Symptoms: sneezing, watery eyes, running nose.
* **Auto Immunity**: Memory-based acquired immunity goes off track and attacks self-cells, causing damage. E.g., Rheumatoid arthritis.
* **Immune System Organs**:
  * **Primary lymphoid organs**: Bone marrow, Thymus. (Sites of maturation and origin).
  * **Secondary lymphoid organs**: Spleen, lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer's patches (Sites of interaction with antigens). MALT (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue) constitutes ~50% of lymphoid tissue.

### AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome)
* Caused by **HIV** (Human Immunodeficiency Virus), a retrovirus with an RNA genome.
* **Transmission**: Unprotected sexual contact, contaminated blood, shared needles, infected mother to child.
* **Mechanism**: HIV enters macrophages $\rightarrow$ viral RNA forms DNA (via reverse transcriptase) $\rightarrow$ viral DNA incorporates into host DNA $\rightarrow$ produces virus particles (macrophage acts as HIV factory). Simultaneously, HIV enters **Helper T-lymphocytes (Th cells)** $\rightarrow$ replicates $\rightarrow$ destroys Th cells. Drastic decrease in Th cell count leads to severe immunodeficiency.
* **Diagnosis**: ELISA (Enzyme Linked Immuno-Sorbent Assay).

### Cancer
* Characterized by uncontrolled cell division. Loss of **contact inhibition** (property of normal cells to stop dividing upon contact with other cells).
* **Tumors**: Benign (confined to origin) and Malignant (proliferating cells called neoplastic cells).
* **Metastasis**: Spread of cancer cells via blood to other sites, forming secondary tumors.
* **Causes (Carcinogens)**: Physical (X-rays, UV rays), Chemical (tobacco smoke), Biological (oncogenic viruses). Activation of proto-oncogenes ($c-onc$).
* **Detection**: Biopsy, histopathology, Radiography (X-rays), CT (Computed Tomography), MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging), Antibodies against cancer-specific antigens.
* **Treatment**: Surgery, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy (using biological response modifiers like $\alpha$-interferons to activate the immune system).

### Drugs and Alcohol Abuse
* **Opioids**: Bind to specific opioid receptors in CNS and GI tract. E.g., Heroin (diacetylmorphine, smack) - depressant, slows body functions. Extracted from latex of poppy plant (*Papaver somniferum*).
* **Cannabinoids**: Interact with receptors in brain. Affect cardiovascular system. From *Cannabis sativa*. Hashish, charas, marijuana, ganja.
* **Coca Alkaloids (Cocaine)**: From *Erythroxylum coca*. Interferes with transport of dopamine. Stimulant, causing euphoria.
* **Adolescence and Addiction**: Characterized by psychological attachment to effects like euphoria (**Addiction**). Body builds **Tolerance** (receptors require higher doses). Sudden cessation causes **Withdrawal syndrome** (anxiety, shakiness, nausea).

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### Life Cycle of Plasmodium
1. **Infection**: Infected female Anopheles mosquito bites human, injecting **sporozoites** (infective stage).
2. **Liver Stage**: Sporozoites travel via blood to the liver, multiply asexually in liver cells, and burst the cells.
3. **Blood Stage**: Parasites enter RBCs, multiply asexually, and burst RBCs, releasing **hemozoin** (causes recurring chills and fever).
4. **Sexual Stage (Human)**: Some parasites differentiate into sexual forms (gametocytes) in RBCs.
5. **Mosquito Uptake**: Female mosquito bites infected human, takes up gametocytes with blood meal.
6. **Mosquito Stage**: Fertilization and development occur in the mosquito's gut.
7. **Maturation**: Mature sporozoites migrate to the mosquito's salivary glands, ready to infect the next host.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Trap Warnings
* **Typhoid Test**: Remember *Widal* test is for Typhoid, not Malaria or Dengue.
* **Antibody for Allergy vs Colostrum**: Allergy involves **IgE**. Colostrum provides **IgA**. Placental transfer is **IgG**.
* **Cellular Immunity vs Humoral**: Cell-mediated immunity (T-cells) is responsible for **graft rejection**. Humoral immunity (B-cells) acts against extracellular pathogens in the blood/lymph.
* **HIV target**: HIV explicitly targets **Helper T-cells** (CD4+ cells) and macrophages, NOT B-cells or Cytotoxic T-cells directly.

### Distinction Table

| Feature | Active Immunity | Passive Immunity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | Produced by host's own body | Received readymade from outside |
| **Time taken** | Slow response | Immediate relief |
| **Memory Cells** | Produced | Not produced |
| **Example** | Vaccination, Natural infection | Colostrum (IgA), Anti-venom |

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

**Problem 1 (Objective/NEET):** Transplant of tissues/organs to save certain patients often fails due to rejection of such tissues/organs by the patient. Which type of immune response is responsible for such rejections?
(A) Auto-immune response
(B) Humoral immune response
(C) Physiological immune response
(D) Cell-mediated immune response
**Speed Method:** Organ rejection is carried out by T-lymphocytes directly attacking the foreign tissue. This is Cell-Mediated Immunity.
*Answer:* (D) Cell-mediated immune response.

**Problem 2 (Subjective/ISC):** A person shows symptoms of continuous high fever (39°C to 40°C), weakness, stomach pain, and constipation.
(i) Identify the disease.
(ii) Name the causative organism.
(iii) Name the test used for diagnosis. (3 marks)
**Step-by-Step Method:**
1. Analyze symptoms: Sustained high fever and gastrointestinal issues strongly point to Typhoid.
2. Identify pathogen: *Salmonella typhi* is the bacterial pathogen.
3. Identify test: The confirmatory test is the Widal test.
*Answer:* (i) Typhoid. (ii) *Salmonella typhi*. (iii) Widal test.
