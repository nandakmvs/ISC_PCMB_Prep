# Chapter 8: Human Health and Diseases

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology
- **Health**: State of complete physical, mental, and social well-being, not merely the absence of disease.
- **Disease**: Any condition which interferes with the normal functioning of the body and impairs health.
- **Pathogens**: Disease-causing organisms (bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoans, helminths).
- **Common Diseases in Humans**:
  - **Bacterial Diseases**:
    - **Typhoid**: Caused by *Salmonella typhi*. Pathogen enters small intestine through contaminated food/water and migrates to other organs via blood. Symptoms: Sustained high fever ($39^{\circ} - 40^{\circ}C$), weakness, stomach pain, constipation, headache, loss of appetite. Intestinal perforation and death may occur in severe cases. Confirmed by **Widal test**.
    - **Pneumonia**: Caused by *Streptococcus pneumoniae* and *Haemophilus influenzae*. Infects alveoli of lungs. Alveoli get filled with fluid leading to severe problems in respiration. Symptoms: fever, chills, cough, headache. In severe cases, lips and finger nails may turn grey to bluish.
  - **Viral Diseases**:
    - **Common Cold**: Caused by **Rhino viruses**. Infect the nose and respiratory passage but **not the lungs**. Symptoms: nasal congestion and discharge, sore throat, hoarseness, cough, headache, tiredness (lasts 3-7 days).
  - **Protozoan Diseases**:
    - **Malaria**: Caused by *Plasmodium* (P. vivax, P. malaria, and P. falciparum). *P. falciparum* causes malignant and fatal malaria. Vector: Female *Anopheles* mosquito.
    - **Amoebiasis (Amoebic dysentery)**: Caused by *Entamoeba histolytica* in the large intestine. Symptoms: constipation, abdominal pain and cramps, stools with excess mucous and blood clots. Vector: Houseflies (mechanical carriers).
  - **Helminthic Diseases**:
    - **Ascariasis**: Caused by *Ascaris* (common roundworm). Symptoms: internal bleeding, muscular pain, fever, anemia, blockage of the intestinal passage.
    - **Elephantiasis (Filariasis)**: Caused by *Wuchereria* (*W. bancrofti* and *W. malayi*). Slowly developing chronic inflammation of organs, usually lymphatic vessels of lower limbs. Genital organs may also be affected. Vector: Female *Culex* mosquito.
  - **Fungal Diseases**:
    - **Ringworm**: Caused by *Microsporum*, *Trichophyton*, and *Epidermophyton*. Symptoms: appearance of dry, scaly lesions on skin, nails, and scalp accompanied by intense itching. Thrives in heat and moisture (e.g., skin folds).
- **Immunity**: The overall ability of the host to fight the disease-causing organisms.
  - **Innate Immunity**: Non-specific defense, present at the time of birth. 
    - **Physical barriers**: Skin, mucus coating of respiratory, gastrointestinal, and urogenital tracts.
    - **Physiological barriers**: Acid in stomach, saliva in mouth, tears from eyes.
    - **Cellular barriers**: Polymorpho-nuclear leukocytes (PMNL-neutrophils), monocytes, natural killer (NK) lymphocytes in the blood, and macrophages in tissues.
    - **Cytokine barriers**: Virus-infected cells secrete proteins called **interferons** which protect non-infected cells from further viral infection.
  - **Acquired Immunity**: Pathogen-specific. Characterized by memory. Primary response (low intensity) and Secondary/Anamnestic response (high intensity).
    - **Humoral Immune Response**: Mediated by **B-lymphocytes** producing antibodies in the blood.
    - **Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI)**: Mediated by **T-lymphocytes**. Responsible for **graft rejection** as the body can differentiate 'self' and 'non-self'.
  - **Active vs Passive Immunity**: 
    - **Active**: Antibodies are produced in the host body in response to antigens (slow). Example: vaccination, natural infection.
    - **Passive**: Ready-made antibodies are directly given to protect the body against foreign agents. Example: Colostrum (IgA), anti-tetanus injection.
- **Allergies**: Exaggerated response of the immune system to certain antigens present in the environment (allergens). Antibodies produced are of **IgE** type. Caused due to the release of chemicals like **histamine** and **serotonin** from **mast cells**. 
- **Autoimmunity**: Due to genetic and other unknown reasons, the body attacks self-cells, resulting in damage to the body. Example: **Rheumatoid arthritis**.
- **Immune System in the Body**:
  - **Primary Lymphoid Organs**: Where immature lymphocytes differentiate into antigen-sensitive lymphocytes.
    - **Bone Marrow**: Main lymphoid organ where all blood cells including lymphocytes are produced. B and T cells originate here, and B cells mature here.
    - **Thymus**: Lobed organ near the heart. Large at birth but keeps reducing in size with age. Site of T-lymphocyte maturation.
  - **Secondary Lymphoid Organs**: Provide the sites for interaction of lymphocytes with the antigen, which then proliferate to become effector cells. Examples: Spleen (large bean-shaped organ containing lymphocytes and phagocytes, graveyard of RBCs), lymph nodes, tonsils, Peyer’s patches of small intestine, and appendix.
  - **MALT (Mucosa-Associated Lymphoid Tissue)**: Located within the lining of major tracts (respiratory, digestive, urogenital). Constitutes about **50%** of the lymphoid tissue in human body.
- **AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome)**: Caused by **HIV** (Human Immunodeficiency Virus), a retrovirus. Transmitted via sexual contact, contaminated blood/needles, or from infected mother to child through placenta. HIV enters **macrophages** (which act like an HIV factory) and **helper T-lymphocytes** ($T_H$). Progressive decrease in $T_H$ cells leads to severe immunodeficiency. Diagnostic test: **ELISA** (Enzyme Linked Immuno-Sorbent Assay). Treatment with anti-retroviral drugs is only partially effective.
- **Cancer**: One of the most dreaded diseases. Breakdown of regulatory mechanisms. Normal cells show **contact inhibition**, which cancer cells lose, leading to uncontrolled division and formation of tumors.
  - **Tumors**: 
    - **Benign**: Remain confined to original location, cause little damage.
    - **Malignant**: Mass of proliferating cells (neoplastic/tumor cells). Grow rapidly, invading surrounding tissues. Escaped cells reach distant sites via blood and start new tumors (**metastasis**).
  - **Causes**: Carcinogens (Physical - X-rays, UV rays; Chemical - tobacco smoke; Biological - oncogenic viruses having viral oncogenes).
  - **Detection**: Biopsy and histopathological studies, radiography, CT (Computed Tomography - uses X-rays for 3D image), MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging - uses magnetic fields and non-ionizing radiation), antibodies against cancer-specific antigens.
  - **Treatment**: Surgery, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, chemotherapy. Biological response modifiers like **$\alpha$-interferon** activate the immune system to destroy the tumor.
- **Drugs and Alcohol Abuse**:
  - **Opioids**: Bind to specific opioid receptors in CNS and gastrointestinal tract. Example: **Heroin** (smack) is chemically **diacetylmorphine**, a white, odorless, bitter crystalline compound formed by acetylation of morphine (extracted from latex of poppy plant *Papaver somniferum*). It is a depressant and slows down body functions.
  - **Cannabinoids**: Interact with cannabinoid receptors in the brain. Obtained from inflorescences of the plant *Cannabis sativa*. Include marijuana, hashish, charas, and ganja. Known for effects on the cardiovascular system.
  - **Coca Alkaloids (Cocaine)**: Obtained from coca plant *Erythroxylum coca*. Interferes with the transport of the neurotransmitter dopamine. Potent stimulating action on CNS, producing euphoria and increased energy. Excessive dosage causes hallucinations.
  - **Adolescence and Addiction**: Characterized by **tolerance** (receptor levels increase, requiring higher doses), **dependence** (tendency of the body to manifest a characteristic and unpleasant withdrawal syndrome if regular dose of drugs/alcohol is abruptly discontinued).
## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### Mechanism 1: Life Cycle of *Plasmodium* (Malaria)
The life cycle requires two hosts: a human and a mosquito.

1. **Infection (Human Host)**: An infected female *Anopheles* mosquito bites a human, injecting **sporozoites** (the infectious stage) into the human's bloodstream along with its saliva.
2. **Hepatic Schizogony (Liver Stage)**: The sporozoites reach the liver through the blood within half an hour. They multiply asexually within the liver cells (hepatocytes), eventually bursting the cells and releasing thousands of parasites into the blood.
3. **Erythrocytic Schizogony (RBC Stage)**: The released parasites attack Red Blood Cells (RBCs), where they multiply asexually. The infected RBCs rupture, releasing more parasites to infect new RBCs.
4. **Release of Haemozoin**: The rupture of RBCs is accompanied by the release of a toxic substance called **haemozoin**. This toxin is responsible for the characteristic chill and high recurring fever (every 3-4 days).
5. **Gametocytogenesis**: After repeated asexual cycles, some of the parasites differentiate into sexual stages called **gametocytes** (male and female) within the red blood cells.
6. **Transmission to Mosquito**: When another female *Anopheles* mosquito takes a blood meal from the infected person, it ingests the gametocytes along with the blood.
7. **Fertilization and Development (Mosquito Host)**: Inside the mosquito's gut (intestine), the gametocytes mature and fertilization occurs. The zygote develops into a motile stage that penetrates the gut wall.
8. **Sporogony**: These mature into infective stages (sporozoites) which escape from the intestine and migrate to the mosquito's **salivary glands**. When this mosquito bites another human, the sporozoites are injected, restarting the cycle.

### Mechanism 2: Replication of HIV (Retrovirus)
HIV targets specific cells of the human immune system, specifically macrophages and Helper T-lymphocytes.

1. **Attachment and Entry**: The virus enters the body and attaches to the surface receptors of a macrophage. The viral protein coat is left outside, and the viral RNA core enters the host cell.
2. **Reverse Transcription**: Inside the macrophage, the viral RNA genome undergoes reverse transcription to form viral DNA. This is catalyzed by the viral enzyme **reverse transcriptase**.
3. **Integration**: The newly synthesized viral DNA enters the host cell nucleus and incorporates itself into the host cell's DNA.
4. **Transcription and Translation**: The integrated viral DNA directs the infected host cell's machinery to transcribe viral RNA and translate viral proteins.
5. **Assembly (Macrophage as HIV Factory)**: New viral particles are assembled. The macrophage survives and continues to produce virus particles, effectively acting as an **HIV factory**.
6. **Infection of Helper T-Cells ($T_H$)**: The newly released viruses enter Helper T-lymphocytes ($T_H$), where they replicate and produce more progeny viruses. 
7. **Immunodeficiency**: The infected $T_H$ cells are destroyed. Repeated attacks cause a progressive decrease in the number of Helper T-lymphocytes in the body. This leads to severe loss of immunity, making the person highly susceptible to opportunistic infections (e.g., *Mycobacterium*, *Toxoplasma*, viruses, fungi) that a healthy immune system would normally defeat.

### Mechanism 3: The Allergic Response
1. **Sensitization (First Exposure)**: Upon first exposure to an allergen (e.g., pollen, dust), the body's immune system identifies it as a threat and produces specific **IgE antibodies**.
2. **Binding to Mast Cells**: These IgE antibodies bind to the surface receptors of **mast cells** and basophils.
3. **Subsequent Exposure**: When the body encounters the same allergen again, the allergen binds to the IgE antibodies attached to the mast cells.
4. **Degranulation**: The binding cross-links the IgE antibodies, triggering the mast cells to degranulate, releasing inflammatory chemicals primarily **histamine** and **serotonin**.
5. **Inflammation**: These chemicals cause vasodilation (widening of blood vessels) and increased vascular permeability, leading to the symptoms of an allergic reaction (sneezing, watery eyes, running nose, difficulty breathing).
## 3. Diagram Blueprints & Labeling Checklists

### Blueprint 1: Structure of an Antibody Molecule (Immunoglobulin)
*When asked to draw or label the structure of an antibody molecule, ensure the following features are clear:*
- **Shape**: A distinct Y-shaped structure.
- **Polypeptide Chains**: Show four peptide chains.
- **Heavy Chains (H)**: Two identical, longer inner chains.
- **Light Chains (L)**: Two identical, shorter outer chains located alongside the top half of the heavy chains.
- **Formula Representation**: Explicitly label the molecule as $H_2L_2$.
- **Bonds**: Draw lines representing **Disulfide bonds (-S-S-)** linking the heavy chain to the light chain, and linking the two heavy chains together in the hinge region.
- **Variable Region / Antigen-Binding Site**: Mark the tips of the 'Y' (the N-terminal ends of both H and L chains) as the antigen-binding site (paratope). Use varied shading or jagged lines to indicate specificity.
- **Constant Region**: The stem and lower parts of the arms.
- **ISC Labeling Checklist**: 
  - [ ] Light chain
  - [ ] Heavy chain
  - [ ] Disulfide bond (-S-S-)
  - [ ] Antigen binding site
  - [ ] Variable region
  - [ ] Constant region

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Trap 1: Active vs. Passive Immunity
**NEET/JEE Objective Trap**: Colostrum is natural, so students often mistake it for "Active" immunity. Remember: *Who produced the antibodies?* If the mother produced it and gave it to the child, it is **Passive**. If your own body made it, it is **Active**.
| Feature | Active Immunity | Passive Immunity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Host's own immune system produces antibodies in response to an antigen. | Ready-made antibodies are directly given to the body. |
| **Speed of Action** | Slow. Takes time to develop a full, effective response. | Fast. Provides immediate relief and protection. |
| **Immunological Memory** | Memory cells are produced. Provides long-lasting protection. | No memory cells are produced. Short-lived protection. |
| **Examples (Natural)** | Immunity acquired after a natural infection (e.g., getting chickenpox). | Antibodies passed from mother to fetus (placenta - IgG) or via colostrum (IgA). |
| **Examples (Artificial)**| Vaccination (e.g., Polio drops, BCG). | Anti-tetanus serum (ATS), Anti-venom for snake bites. |

### Trap 2: Innate vs. Acquired Immunity
| Feature | Innate Immunity | Acquired Immunity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Specificity** | Non-specific (works against all pathogens generally). | Pathogen-specific (tailored response for specific microbes). |
| **Time of Presence** | Present from birth. | Acquired over a lifetime after exposure. |
| **Memory** | Lacks immunological memory. | Characterized by immunological memory. |
| **Components** | Physical, physiological, cellular, and cytokine barriers. | B-lymphocytes (Humoral) and T-lymphocytes (Cell-mediated). |

### Trap 3: Benign vs. Malignant Tumors
| Feature | Benign Tumor | Malignant Tumor |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Growth & Localization** | Slow growing. Remains restricted to the original location. | Rapidly growing. Invades and damages surrounding normal tissues. |
| **Capsule** | Usually enclosed in a connective tissue capsule. | Not enclosed; cells continuously break loose. |
| **Metastasis** | Do not show metastasis. | Show metastasis (spreads to distant sites via blood). |
| **Severity** | Cause little damage; generally non-fatal. | Fatal if untreated (cancer). |

### Trap 4: Humoral vs. Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI)
**ISC Subjective Trap**: When asked about organ transplant rejection, do NOT just say "immunity rejects it." You must explicitly state **Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI)** and mention **T-lymphocytes**.
| Feature | Humoral Immunity | Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mediating Cells** | B-lymphocytes. | T-lymphocytes. |
| **Mechanism** | Produces antibodies that circulate in blood and lymph. | Directly attacks infected cells, cancer cells, and foreign tissue. |
| **Primary Target** | Extracellular pathogens (bacteria, viruses in blood). | Intracellular pathogens, transplanted organs (graft rejection). |

### Subjective Warning for ISC Students
- **Writing Pathogen Names**: You will lose marks if you do not strictly follow binomial nomenclature rules. Always underline scientific names of pathogens separately (e.g., _Plasmodium_ _falciparum_).
- **Defining Haemozoin**: Don't just say "it causes fever". Explicitly state that it is a *toxic substance* released upon the *rupture of RBCs* that is responsible for the characteristic recurring chills and fever in malaria.
- **Antibody Representation**: Always write $H_2L_2$ for the structure of an antibody molecule when prompted. Mentioning disulfide bonds is non-negotiable for full marks.
## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

### Problem 1 (ISC Subjective 3-Marker): Explain the role of macrophages in providing immunity and their involvement in HIV infection.
**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. **Phagocytosis (Innate Immunity)**: Macrophages are large, phagocytic white blood cells present in tissues. They engulf and destroy invading microbes and cellular debris, acting as a crucial cellular barrier of innate immunity.
2. **Antigen Presentation (Bridging Immunity)**: After digesting a pathogen, macrophages display pieces of the pathogen (antigens) on their cell surface. This presents the antigen to Helper T-cells, triggering acquired immunity.
3. **HIV Factory (Pathology)**: During HIV infection, the virus specifically targets macrophages. The viral RNA enters the macrophage, undergoes reverse transcription to form DNA, and integrates into the host genome. The macrophage survives and continuously produces new virus particles, effectively acting as an **HIV factory**.

### Problem 2 (NEET/JEE Objective Speed Method): Which of the following immune responses is responsible for the rejection of a kidney graft?
(A) Auto-immune response
(B) Humoral immune response
(C) Inflammatory immune response
(D) Cell-mediated immune response
**Speed Logic**: "Graft rejection" or "Transplant rejection" is always triggered by the body recognizing tissue as "non-self". The branch of immunity responsible for distinguishing self from non-self at a cellular level and attacking foreign cells is Cell-Mediated Immunity (CMI). CMI = T-lymphocytes.
**Answer**: (D)

### Problem 3 (ISC Subjective 2-Marker): Why is colostrum considered essential for a newborn infant? State the type of immunity it provides.
**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. **Definition and Content**: Colostrum is the yellowish fluid secreted by the mammary glands during the initial days of lactation. It is highly essential because it contains abundant antibodies, specifically **IgA**.
2. **Type of Immunity**: These maternal antibodies provide **Natural Passive Immunity** to the newborn, offering immediate protection against various infections during the early stages of life when the infant's own immune system is immature.

### Problem 4 (NEET/JEE Objective Trap): A patient is bitten by a highly venomous snake and brought to the hospital. The doctor immediately injects anti-venom. What type of immunity is the doctor providing?
(A) Artificial Active Immunity
(B) Artificial Passive Immunity
(C) Natural Active Immunity
(D) Natural Passive Immunity
**Speed Logic**: 
- Is the body making the antibodies? No, they are injected. Therefore, it is **Passive**.
- Is it acquired through natural biological processes (like mother to fetus)? No, it is a medical injection. Therefore, it is **Artificial**.
**Answer**: (B)

### Problem 5 (ISC Subjective 5-Marker): Describe the life cycle of *Plasmodium* in the human host, starting from the bite of an infected mosquito.
**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. **Inoculation**: An infected female *Anopheles* mosquito bites a human and injects **sporozoites** (the infective stage) into the blood.
2. **Liver Stage (Hepatic Schizogony)**: The sporozoites travel to the liver, where they multiply asexually inside hepatocytes. This multiplication causes the liver cells to burst, releasing the parasites into the bloodstream.
3. **Blood Stage (Erythrocytic Schizogony)**: The released parasites infect Red Blood Cells (RBCs). They multiply asexually within the RBCs, causing them to rupture.
4. **Symptom Generation**: The rupture of RBCs is accompanied by the release of a toxic substance called **haemozoin**, which is responsible for the characteristic recurring chills and high fever.
5. **Sexual Stage**: After several cycles of asexual reproduction, some parasites in the RBCs differentiate into sexual stages called **gametocytes** (male and female), which will be ingested by another mosquito during a blood meal to complete the cycle.
