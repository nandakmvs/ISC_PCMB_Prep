# Chapter 07: Evolution (Till Hardy-Weinberg Principle)

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

### Origin of Life
* **Big Bang Theory**: Explains the origin of the universe (~20 billion years ago).
* Earth formed ~4.5 billion years ago. Early atmosphere had no oxygen; contained water vapor, $CH_4, CO_2, NH_3$. UV rays broke water into H2 and O2.
* **Theory of Spontaneous Generation**: Life came from decaying and rotting matter. Louis Pasteur disproved this with his swan-neck flask experiment.
* **Chemical Evolution (Oparin-Haldane Theory)**: Life originated from pre-existing non-living organic molecules (RNA, protein, etc.), and formation of life was preceded by chemical evolution.
* **Miller-Urey Experiment (1953)**: Created conditions of primitive earth (high temperature, volcanic storms, reducing atmosphere containing $CH_4, NH_3, H_2$, and water vapour at 800°C) in a lab. Observed formation of **amino acids**.
* **First Life**: Non-cellular life forms (giant molecules like RNA, Proteins, Polysaccharides) originated ~3 billion years ago. Cellular life forms ~2 billion years ago.

### Evolution of Life Forms - A Theory
* **Charles Darwin**: Journeyed on H.M.S. Beagle. Proposed **Natural Selection** as the mechanism of evolution. Two key concepts: Branching descent and natural selection.
* **Alfred Wallace**: Worked in the Malay Archipelago and arrived at similar conclusions independently.
* Thomas Malthus' work on population influenced Darwin.

### Evidences for Evolution
1. **Paleontological Evidence**: Study of fossils. Different aged rock sediments contain fossils of different life-forms, showing life evolved over time.
2. **Embryological Evidence**: Proposed by Ernst Haeckel (ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny), but disproved by **Karl Ernst von Baer**, who showed embryos never pass through the adult stages of other animals.
3. **Morphological and Anatomical Evidence**:
   * **Homologous Organs**: Same structure/origin, different functions. Indicate **Divergent Evolution**. e.g., Forelimbs of whales, bats, cheetahs, and humans; vertebrate hearts/brains; Thorn of *Bougainvillea* and tendril of *Cucurbita*.
   * **Analogous Organs**: Different structure/origin, similar functions. Indicate **Convergent Evolution**. e.g., Wings of butterfly and bird; eyes of octopus and mammals; flippers of penguins and dolphins; Sweet potato (root) and potato (stem).
4. **Biochemical Evidence**: Similarities in proteins and genes among diverse organisms show common ancestry.

### What is Adaptive Radiation?
* The process of evolution of different species in a given geographical area starting from a point and radiating to other areas of geography (habitats).
* **Examples**: Darwin's Finches in the Galapagos Islands (beak shapes adapted to different food). Australian Marsupials.
* **Convergent Evolution in Adaptive Radiation**: When more than one adaptive radiation occurs in an isolated geographical area (e.g., Placental mammals in Australia showing similarities to Australian marsupials, like Placental wolf and Tasmanian wolf).

### Biological Evolution
* According to Darwin, fitness refers ultimately to reproductive fitness.
* **Lamarck's Theory**: Evolution driven by use and disuse of organs (e.g., giraffe's neck). Now obsolete because acquired traits are not inherited.
* **Mutation Theory (Hugo de Vries)**: Worked on evening primrose (*Oenothera lamarckiana*). Stated that large, single-step mutations (**Saltation**) cause speciation. Unlike Darwinian variations (which are minor, continuous, and directional), mutations are random and directionless.

### Hardy-Weinberg Principle
* States that allele frequencies in a population are stable and is constant from generation to generation in the absence of evolutionary influences. This is called **genetic equilibrium**.
* Equation: $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$
  * $p$ = frequency of dominant allele (A)
  * $q$ = frequency of recessive allele (a)
  * $p^2$ = frequency of homozygous dominant (AA)
  * $q^2$ = frequency of homozygous recessive (aa)
  * $2pq$ = frequency of heterozygous (Aa)
* **Five factors that affect Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium**:
  1. Gene migration or gene flow
  2. Genetic drift (random change in allele frequency; can cause a **Founder Effect**)
  3. Mutation
  4. Genetic recombination
  5. Natural selection

### Operation of Natural Selection on Traits
1. **Stabilizing Selection**: More individuals acquire mean character value (peak gets higher and narrower).
2. **Directional Selection**: More individuals acquire values other than the mean (peak shifts in one direction).
3. **Disruptive Selection**: More individuals acquire peripheral character values at both ends (two peaks form).

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### The Hardy-Weinberg Derivation
Let there be two alleles A and a in a population.
1. Frequency of allele A = $p$
2. Frequency of allele a = $q$
3. Since these are the only two alleles, $p + q = 1$.
4. The probability of an individual being AA is $p \times p = p^2$.
5. The probability of an individual being aa is $q \times q = q^2$.
6. The probability of being Aa is $(p \times q) + (q \times p) = 2pq$.
7. Thus, $p^2 + 2pq + q^2 = 1$. This is the binomial expansion of $(p + q)^2 = 1$.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Trap Warnings
* **Homologous vs Analogous Plants**: Remember that sweet potato is a modified root and potato is a modified stem. Since they perform the same function (food storage) but have different origins, they are **analogous**.
* **Founder Effect vs Bottleneck**: Both are forms of genetic drift. Founder effect happens when a small group leaves a population to start a new one.
* **De Vries vs Darwin**: Darwinian variations are continuous and directional. De Vries mutations are discontinuous, random, and directionless.

### Distinction Table

| Feature | Homologous Organs | Analogous Organs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin / Structure** | Same basic structural plan | Different structural plan |
| **Function** | May perform different functions | Perform similar functions |
| **Evolution Type** | Divergent Evolution | Convergent Evolution |
| **Examples** | Bat wing, human arm, whale flipper | Bird wing, insect wing |

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

**Problem 1 (Objective/NEET):** In a population in Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium, the frequency of a recessive allele is 0.4. What is the frequency of heterozygotes in this population?
**Speed Method:**
1. Recessive allele ($q$) = 0.4
2. Dominant allele ($p$) = $1 - 0.4 = 0.6$
3. Heterozygote frequency = $2pq = 2 \times 0.6 \times 0.4 = 0.48$.
*Answer:* 48% or 0.48.

**Problem 2 (Subjective/ISC):** Differentiate between divergent and convergent evolution giving one example of each. (3 marks)
**Step-by-Step Method:**
1. Define **Divergent Evolution**: Evolutionary process where related species develop different traits due to adaptation to different environments. This leads to homologous structures.
2. Example: Forelimbs of mammals (whales for swimming, bats for flying, cheetahs for running) have the same bone structure (humerus, radius, ulna, carpals) but different functions.
3. Define **Convergent Evolution**: Process where unrelated species independently evolve similar traits to adapt to similar environments or ecological niches. This leads to analogous structures.
4. Example: Wings of a butterfly and wings of a bird. They have completely different anatomical structures but serve the same function (flight).
