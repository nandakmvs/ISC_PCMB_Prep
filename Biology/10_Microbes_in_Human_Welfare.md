# Chapter 10: Microbes in Human Welfare

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

Microbes are diverse—protozoa, bacteria, fungi, and microscopic animal and plant viruses, viroids, and also prions that are proteinaceous infectious agents. While some microbes cause diseases, many are highly beneficial to humans.

### 1.1 Microbes in Household Products
*   **Curd**: Micro-organisms such as **Lactobacillus** and others commonly called **Lactic Acid Bacteria (LAB)** grow in milk and convert it to curd. During growth, LAB produce acids that **coagulate and partially digest the milk proteins**. A small amount of curd added to fresh milk as **inoculum** or **starter** contains millions of LAB, which at suitable temperatures multiply, thus converting milk to curd. It also **improves its nutritional quality by increasing vitamin B12**. In our stomach, LAB play a very beneficial role in **checking disease-causing microbes**.
*   **Dough**: The dough used for making foods such as **dosa and idli** is fermented by bacteria. The puffed-up appearance of dough is due to the production of **CO2 gas**. The dough used for making bread is fermented using **baker's yeast** (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*).
*   **Toddy**: A traditional drink of some parts of southern India is made by fermenting sap from palms.
*   **Cheese**: Different varieties of cheese are known by their characteristic texture, flavour, and taste, depending on the microbe used. The large holes in **'Swiss cheese'** are due to production of a large amount of CO2 by a bacterium named ***Propionibacterium sharmanii***. **'Roquefort cheese'** is ripened by growing a specific fungi (*Penicillium roqueforti*) on them, which gives them a particular flavour.

### 1.2 Microbes in Industrial Products
For industrial purposes, microbes are grown in very large vessels called **fermentors**.
*   **Fermented Beverages**: ***Saccharomyces cerevisiae*** (brewer's yeast) is used for fermenting malted cereals and fruit juices to produce **ethanol**. Depending on the type of the raw material and the type of processing (with or without distillation), different alcoholic drinks are obtained. Wine and beer are produced **without distillation**, whereas whisky, brandy, and rum are produced by **distillation** of the fermented broth.
*   **Antibiotics**: These are chemical substances produced by some microbes that can kill or retard the growth of other (disease-causing) microbes. **Penicillin** was the first antibiotic discovered (by Alexander Fleming from the mould ***Penicillium notatum***). Its full potential as an effective antibiotic was established much later by **Ernest Chain and Howard Florey**. 
*   **Chemicals, Enzymes, and Bioactive Molecules**:
    *   **Organic Acids**: ***Aspergillus niger*** (fungus) for **Citric acid**; ***Acetobacter aceti*** (bacterium) for **Acetic acid**; ***Clostridium butylicum*** (bacterium) for **Butyric acid**; ***Lactobacillus*** (bacterium) for **Lactic acid**.
    *   **Enzymes**: **Lipases** (used in detergent formulations to remove oily stains); **Pectinases and Proteases** (used to clarify bottled fruit juices).
    *   **Bioactive Molecules**: **Streptokinase** (produced by bacterium *Streptococcus*, modified by genetic engineering) is used as a **'clot buster'** for removing clots from the blood vessels of patients who have undergone myocardial infarction. **Cyclosporin A** (produced by fungus ***Trichoderma polysporum***) is used as an **immunosuppressive agent** in organ-transplant patients. **Statins** (produced by yeast ***Monascus purpureus***) act as **blood-cholesterol lowering agents** by competitively inhibiting the enzyme responsible for cholesterol synthesis.

### 1.3 Microbes in Sewage Treatment
Sewage contains large amounts of organic matter and microbes. Treatment is done in **Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs)** to make it less polluting.
*   **Primary Treatment**: Physical removal of particles through **filtration and sedimentation**. Floating debris is removed by sequential filtration, and grit (soil and small pebbles) by sedimentation. All solids that settle form the **primary sludge**, and the supernatant forms the **effluent**.
*   **Secondary Treatment (Biological Treatment)**: The primary effluent is passed into large **aeration tanks** where it is constantly agitated, allowing vigorous growth of useful aerobic microbes into **flocs** (masses of bacteria associated with fungal filaments to form mesh-like structures). These microbes consume a major part of the **organic matter**, significantly reducing the **BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand)**. **BOD** is the amount of oxygen consumed if all the organic matter in one litre of water were oxidized by bacteria. Lower BOD means less polluting potential.
*   After BOD is reduced, the effluent is passed into a settling tank where the flocs sediment, producing **activated sludge**. A small part is pumped back as inoculum, while the major part goes to large tanks called **anaerobic sludge digesters**. Here, anaerobic bacteria digest the bacteria and fungi, producing a mixture of gases (**methane, hydrogen sulphide, and carbon dioxide**) known as **biogas**. The effluent is finally released into natural water bodies.

### 1.4 Microbes in Production of Biogas
Biogas is a mixture of gases (predominantly methane) produced by the microbial activity. Bacteria that grow anaerobically on cellulosic material produce large amounts of **methane (CH4)** along with CO2 and H2. These are called **methanogens** (e.g., ***Methanobacterium***). They are present in the anaerobic sludge and in the **rumen** of cattle (helping in cellulose breakdown). Thus, cattle dung (**gobar**) is rich in these bacteria and is used to generate biogas (**gobar gas**).

### 1.5 Microbes as Biocontrol Agents
Biocontrol refers to the use of biological methods for controlling plant diseases and pests.
*   **Ladybird and Dragonflies**: Useful to get rid of aphids and mosquitoes, respectively.
*   ***Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt)**: Used to control butterfly caterpillars. Available as dried spores mixed with water and sprayed onto vulnerable plants. In the gut of the larvae, the toxin is released, killing the larvae. Genetic engineering has led to plants like **Bt cotton** which are resistant to insect pests.
*   ***Trichoderma***: Free-living fungi found in root ecosystems, effective against several plant pathogens.
*   **Baculoviruses**: Pathogens that attack insects and other arthropods. The majority used as biocontrol agents are in the genus ***Nucleopolyhedrovirus***. These have species-specific, narrow-spectrum insecticidal applications and have **no negative impacts on plants, mammals, birds, fish, or even on non-target insects** (highly desirable for **Integrated Pest Management (IPM)** programs).

### 1.6 Microbes as Biofertilisers
Biofertilisers are organisms that enrich the nutrient quality of the soil.
*   **Bacteria**: The nodules on the roots of leguminous plants are formed by the symbiotic association of ***Rhizobium***, which fixes atmospheric nitrogen into organic forms. Free-living bacteria in the soil like ***Azospirillum*** and ***Azotobacter*** also fix nitrogen.
*   **Fungi (Mycorrhiza)**: Symbiotic associations between fungi (many members of genus ***Glomus***) and plants. The fungus absorbs phosphorus from soil and passes it to the plant, and also provides resistance to root-borne pathogens and tolerance to salinity and drought.
*   **Cyanobacteria**: Autotrophic microbes widely distributed in aquatic and terrestrial environments, many capable of fixing atmospheric nitrogen (e.g., ***Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria***). In paddy fields, cyanobacteria serve as an important biofertiliser. They also add organic matter to the soil and increase its fertility.
## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### 2.1 Mechanism of Sewage Treatment (STP Workings)

The transformation of highly polluting sewage into less harmful effluent is a rigorous, multi-step process in STPs. 

**Step 1: Primary Treatment (Physical)**
1.  **Sequential Filtration**: Raw sewage first passes through screens. Large floating debris (leaves, rags, plastics, animal matter) is physically removed.
2.  **Sedimentation**: The filtered sewage enters a grit chamber where the speed of flow is reduced. Grit (soil, sand, and small pebbles) settles down by gravity.
3.  **Resultant Fractionation**: The settled solid materials form the **primary sludge**. The supernatant liquid (liquid on top) becomes the **primary effluent**. 

**Step 2: Secondary Treatment (Biological)**
1.  **Aeration**: The primary effluent is pumped into large aeration tanks. It is mechanically agitated and air is pumped into it.
2.  **Floc Formation**: This vigorous aeration allows rapid growth of useful aerobic microbes. They form **flocs**—masses of bacteria surrounded by slime and associated with fungal filaments to form a mesh-like structure.
3.  **BOD Reduction**: While growing in these flocs, the microbes rapidly consume the major part of the organic matter in the effluent. This significantly reduces the **Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD)**. *The greater the BOD of wastewater, the more is its polluting potential.*
4.  **Settling**: Once BOD is significantly reduced, the effluent is passed into a settling tank. The bacterial flocs are allowed to sediment. This sediment is now called **activated sludge**.

**Step 3: Sludge Digestion (Anaerobic)**
1.  **Inoculum Return**: A small part of the activated sludge is pumped back into the aeration tank to serve as an **inoculum** (starter).
2.  **Anaerobic Sludge Digestion**: The remaining major part of the activated sludge is pumped into large tanks called **anaerobic sludge digesters**.
3.  **Biogas Production**: Here, other kinds of bacteria, which grow anaerobically, digest the bacteria and fungi in the sludge. During this digestion, they produce a mixture of gases such as **methane, hydrogen sulphide, and carbon dioxide**. These gases form **biogas**, which can be used as a source of energy as it is inflammable.

**Step 4: Final Discharge**
1.  The treated effluent from the secondary treatment plant is finally released into natural water bodies like rivers and streams.

### 2.2 Mechanism of Biogas Production in a Biogas Plant

A biogas plant is practically applied in rural areas to utilize cattle dung (gobar). 

**Step 1: Slurry Preparation**
1.  A concrete tank (10-15 feet deep) is constructed.
2.  Bio-wastes and a slurry of dung and water are collected and fed into this tank.

**Step 2: Anaerobic Digestion**
1.  A floating cover is placed over the slurry.
2.  **Methanogens** (e.g., *Methanobacterium*) present in the dung start digesting the cellulose and organic matter anaerobically.
3.  The digestion process proceeds in three stages:
    *   **Solubilization**: Complex insoluble polymers (cellulose) are converted into simple soluble monomers.
    *   **Acidogenesis**: Monomers are converted into organic acids (acetic acid).
    *   **Methanogenesis**: Acetic acid is converted by methanogens into methane (CH4) and CO2.

**Step 3: Gas Collection and Slurry Removal**
1.  As the methanogens produce biogas (CH4, CO2, H2), the gas accumulates, causing the floating cover to rise upwards.
2.  The biogas plant has an outlet, which is connected to a pipe to supply biogas to nearby houses for cooking and lighting.
3.  The spent slurry is removed through another outlet and can be used as an excellent, nutrient-rich **fertiliser**.

### 2.3 Mechanism of Action of *Bacillus thuringiensis* (Bt)

**Step 1: Spraying/Ingestion**
1.  Spores of Bt (available in sachets as dry powder) are mixed with water and sprayed onto vulnerable plants (like brassicas and fruit trees).
2.  Insect larvae (caterpillars) eat the plant parts and ingest the Bt spores.

**Step 2: Toxin Activation in Gut**
1.  The spores contain a toxic insecticidal protein which exists as inactive protoxins.
2.  Once ingested, the **alkaline pH** of the insect gut solubilizes the crystals and converts the inactive protoxin into an active toxin.

**Step 3: Cell Lysis and Death**
1.  The activated toxin binds to the surface of midgut epithelial cells and creates pores.
2.  This causes cell swelling and lysis, eventually leading to the death of the insect larva.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### 4.1 Objective vs Subjective Traps

*   **TRAP 1: Primary vs Activated Sludge.** 
    *   *Primary sludge* is simply the physical dirt/grit/pebbles that settle during primary treatment. It contains NO flocs.
    *   *Activated sludge* is formed during secondary treatment. It is highly biological, consisting of sedimented **flocs** (bacteria + fungal filaments). 
*   **TRAP 2: Aerobic vs Anaerobic phases in STP.**
    *   BOD reduction happens because of *aerobic* bacteria in the Aeration Tank.
    *   Biogas production happens because of *anaerobic* bacteria in the Anaerobic Sludge Digester. Do NOT confuse the two tanks.
*   **TRAP 3: Mycorrhiza vs Rhizobium.**
    *   *Rhizobium* forms nodules and fixes Nitrogen.
    *   *Mycorrhiza* (Glomus) does NOT fix nitrogen. It primarily absorbs **Phosphorus** from the soil. NEET heavily tests this distinction.
*   **TRAP 4: Statins vs Streptokinase.**
    *   *Statins* = Lower cholesterol (competitive inhibition). Produced by **Yeast** (*Monascus purpureus*).
    *   *Streptokinase* = Clot buster (dissolves blood clots). Produced by **Bacteria** (*Streptococcus*).
*   **TRAP 5: Distilled vs Undistilled Beverages.**
    *   *Wine and Beer* = NO distillation (low alcohol content).
    *   *Whisky, Brandy, Rum* = Distillation (high alcohol content).
*   **TRAP 6: Baculovirus Target.**
    *   They are species-specific and affect *insects and arthropods*, NOT plants or mammals. This makes them perfect for IPM (Integrated Pest Management).

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

### 5.1 ISC Subjective Model (Step-by-Step)

**Q1. Describe the biological treatment of sewage. How does it reduce BOD? (3 Marks)**

**ISC Step-by-Step Solution:**
*   **Step 1 (Aeration & Flocs):** The primary effluent is passed into large aeration tanks where it is constantly agitated mechanically and air is pumped into it. This allows vigorous growth of useful aerobic microbes into **flocs** (masses of bacteria associated with fungal filaments). *(1 mark)*
*   **Step 2 (BOD Reduction):** While growing, these microbes rapidly consume the major part of the organic matter present in the effluent. This consumption of organic matter significantly reduces the Biochemical Oxygen Demand (BOD) of the effluent. *(1 mark)*
*   **Step 3 (Settling & Activated Sludge):** Once the BOD is reduced, the effluent is passed into a settling tank where the bacterial flocs are allowed to sediment. This sediment is called **activated sludge**. *(1 mark)*

**Q2. What is a broad-spectrum antibiotic? Name the first antibiotic discovered and its source. (2 Marks)**

**ISC Step-by-Step Solution:**
*   **Step 1:** An antibiotic that can kill or destroy a wide range of disease-causing microorganisms (both Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria) is called a broad-spectrum antibiotic. *(1 mark)*
*   **Step 2:** The first antibiotic discovered was **Penicillin**. It was obtained from the fungus ***Penicillium notatum***. *(1 mark)*

**Q3. Explain the role of Baculoviruses as biocontrol agents. Why are they considered excellent candidates for species-specific, narrow spectrum insecticidal applications? (3 Marks)**

**ISC Step-by-Step Solution:**
*   **Step 1 (Pathogen Role):** Baculoviruses (especially of the genus *Nucleopolyhedrovirus*) are pathogens that attack specific insects and other arthropods. *(1 mark)*
*   **Step 2 (No Non-Target Effects):** They are excellent candidates because they have been shown to have NO negative impacts on plants, mammals, birds, fish, or even non-target insects. *(1 mark)*
*   **Step 3 (IPM Application):** This narrow-spectrum, species-specific nature makes them highly desirable for Integrated Pest Management (IPM) programs, where beneficial insects are conserved while target pests are eradicated. *(1 mark)*
