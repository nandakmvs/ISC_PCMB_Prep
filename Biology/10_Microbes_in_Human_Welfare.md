# Chapter 10: Microbes in Human Welfare

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

### Microbes in Household Products
* **Curd**: Lactic Acid Bacteria (**LAB**, e.g., *Lactobacillus*) grow in milk and convert it to curd. LAB produce acids that coagulate and partially digest the milk proteins. A small amount of curd added to fresh milk as **inoculum** or starter contains millions of LAB. Improves nutritional quality by increasing **Vitamin B12**. LAB also play a beneficial role in checking disease-causing microbes in the stomach.
* **Dough**: Used for bread, fermented by baker's yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*). Puffed-up appearance is due to the production of $CO_2$ gas. Dough for dosa and idli is fermented by bacteria.
* **Toddy**: A traditional drink in southern India made by fermenting sap from palms.
* **Cheese**: Different varieties are known by their characteristic texture, flavour, and taste.
  * **Swiss cheese**: Large holes are due to a large amount of $CO_2$ produced by a bacterium named *Propionibacterium sharmanii*.
  * **Roquefort cheese**: Ripened by growing a specific fungus on them, which gives a particular flavour.

### Microbes in Industrial Products
* Grown in very large vessels called **fermentors**.
* **Fermented Beverages**: *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* (Brewer's yeast) ferments malted cereals and fruit juices to produce ethanol.
  * Without distillation: Wine and beer (low alcohol concentration).
  * With distillation of the fermented broth: Whisky, brandy, and rum (high alcohol concentration).
* **Antibiotics**: Chemical substances produced by some microbes that can kill or retard the growth of other (disease-causing) microbes.
  * **Penicillin**: First antibiotic discovered by Alexander Fleming (from fungus *Penicillium notatum*) while working on Staphylococci bacteria. Full potential established by Ernest Chain and Howard Florey.
* **Chemicals, Enzymes and Bioactive Molecules**:
  * **Acids**:
    * Citric acid: *Aspergillus niger* (a fungus)
    * Acetic acid: *Acetobacter aceti* (a bacterium)
    * Butyric acid: *Clostridium butylicum* (a bacterium)
    * Lactic acid: *Lactobacillus* (a bacterium)
  * **Enzymes**:
    * **Lipases**: Used in detergent formulations to remove oily stains from laundry.
    * **Pectinases and Proteases**: Used to clarify bottled fruit juices.
    * **Streptokinase**: Produced by the bacterium *Streptococcus*, modified by genetic engineering to act as a **clot buster** for removing clots from blood vessels of patients suffering from myocardial infarction.
  * **Bioactive Molecules**:
    * **Cyclosporin A**: Used as an **immunosuppressive agent** in organ-transplant patients. Produced by the fungus *Trichoderma polysporum*.
    * **Statins**: Produced by the yeast *Monascus purpureus*. Used as **blood-cholesterol lowering agents** by competitively inhibiting the enzyme responsible for synthesizing cholesterol.

### Microbes in Sewage Treatment
* **Primary Treatment**: Physical removal of particles (large and small) from sewage through **filtration** and **sedimentation**. Forms **primary sludge** and **effluent**.
* **Secondary (Biological) Treatment**:
  * Effluent is passed into large aeration tanks and mechanically agitated. This allows vigorous growth of useful aerobic microbes into **flocs** (masses of bacteria associated with fungal filaments to form mesh-like structures).
  * Microbes consume the major part of organic matter, significantly reducing the **BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand)**. BOD is a measure of the organic matter present in the water.
  * Once BOD is reduced, the effluent is passed into a settling tank where the flocs sediment. This sediment is called **activated sludge**.
* **Anaerobic Sludge Digesters**: A small part of the activated sludge is pumped back to the aeration tank to serve as inoculum. The remaining major part is pumped into large tanks where anaerobic bacteria digest the bacteria and fungi in the sludge. During this digestion, a mixture of gases such as methane, hydrogen sulphide, and carbon dioxide are produced, which form **biogas**.

### Microbes in Production of Biogas
* **Biogas**: A mixture of gases containing predominantly methane, produced by microbial activity.
* **Methanogens**: Bacteria that grow anaerobically on cellulosic material and produce large amounts of methane along with $CO_2$ and $H_2$. Example: *Methanobacterium*. Commonly found in the anaerobic sludge during sewage treatment and in the rumen (part of stomach) of cattle.

### Microbes as Biocontrol Agents
* **Biocontrol**: Use of biological methods for controlling plant diseases and pests.
* Examples:
  * Ladybird and Dragonflies: Get rid of aphids and mosquitoes respectively.
  * **Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt)**: Bacteria introduced to control butterfly caterpillars. Spores are sprayed on plants (e.g., brassicas, fruit trees). In the gut of the larvae, the toxin is released and the larvae get killed.
  * **Trichoderma**: Free-living fungi present in root ecosystems, effective against several plant pathogens.
  * **Baculoviruses**: Pathogens that attack insects and other arthropods. The majority used as biocontrol agents are in the genus **Nucleopolyhedrovirus (NPV)**. They have narrow-spectrum, species-specific insecticidal applications with no negative impacts on non-target organisms.

### Microbes as Biofertilisers
* **Biofertilisers**: Organisms that enrich the nutrient quality of the soil.
* **Bacteria**:
  * Symbiotic: *Rhizobium* in root nodules of leguminous plants fixes atmospheric nitrogen into organic forms.
  * Free-living: *Azospirillum* and *Azotobacter* fix atmospheric nitrogen in the soil.
* **Fungi**:
  * Form symbiotic associations with plants (**mycorrhiza**). Members of the genus *Glomus* form mycorrhiza. Fungi absorb phosphorus from soil and pass it to the plant, provide resistance to root-borne pathogens, tolerance to salinity and drought.
* **Cyanobacteria**: Autotrophic microbes widely distributed in aquatic and terrestrial environments. Fix atmospheric nitrogen (e.g., *Anabaena, Nostoc, Oscillatoria*). Important biofertiliser in paddy fields.

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### Sewage Treatment Process Flow
1. **Raw Sewage** $\rightarrow$ Filtration & Sedimentation (Primary Treatment).
2. Solid waste settles as **Primary Sludge**. Liquid floating part is the **Effluent**.
3. Effluent passed to **Aeration Tanks** (Secondary Treatment).
4. Aerobic bacteria + fungi form **Flocs** $\rightarrow$ consume organic matter $\rightarrow$ lower **BOD**.
5. Once BOD is low, mixture goes to Settling Tank. Flocs settle to form **Activated Sludge**.
6. Small portion of activated sludge sent back to aeration tank (inoculum).
7. Major portion of activated sludge sent to **Anaerobic Sludge Digesters** $\rightarrow$ anaerobic bacteria produce **Biogas**.
8. Treated effluent is discharged into water bodies.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Trap Warnings
* **Cyclosporin A vs Statins**: Very frequently swapped in NEET. *Trichoderma* $\rightarrow$ Cyclosporin A (immunosuppressant). *Monascus purpureus* $\rightarrow$ Statins (blood cholesterol lowering).
* **BOD Meaning**: A **higher** BOD means **more** polluting potential, because it indicates a high amount of organic matter that aerobic microbes will consume oxygen to break down.
* **Primary vs Activated Sludge**: Primary sludge is formed during the *physical* process (sedimentation). Activated sludge is formed by the settling of *biological* flocs after the secondary treatment.

### Distinction Table

| Feature | Primary Sewage Treatment | Secondary Sewage Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature of Process** | Physical (Filtration/Sedimentation) | Biological (Microbial digestion) |
| **Material Removed** | Large and small solid particles | Dissolved and suspended organic matter |
| **Sludge Formed** | Primary Sludge | Activated Sludge (Flocs) |

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

**Problem 1 (Objective/NEET):** Which one of the following is correctly matched?
(A) *Acetobacter aceti* : Antibiotics
(B) *Methanobacterium* : Lactic acid
(C) *Penicillium notatum* : Acetic acid
(D) *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* : Ethanol
**Speed Method:** Use elimination. Acetobacter $\rightarrow$ acetic acid. Methanobacterium $\rightarrow$ biogas. Penicillium $\rightarrow$ antibiotic. Saccharomyces $\rightarrow$ yeast for ethanol/bread.
*Answer:* (D)

**Problem 2 (Subjective/ISC):** Describe the role of microbes in producing bioactive molecules for medical applications. Give three examples with the name of the microbe. (3 marks)
**Step-by-Step Method:**
1. State that microbes synthesize compounds used directly in human medicine.
2. **Example 1**: Streptokinase. Produced by *Streptococcus*, modified by genetic engineering, used as a clot buster for heart attack patients.
3. **Example 2**: Cyclosporin A. Produced by fungus *Trichoderma polysporum*, used as an immunosuppressive agent in organ transplants.
4. **Example 3**: Statins. Produced by yeast *Monascus purpureus*, act as blood-cholesterol lowering agents by inhibiting cholesterol synthesis enzymes.
