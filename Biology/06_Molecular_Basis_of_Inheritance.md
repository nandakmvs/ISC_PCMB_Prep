# 06. Molecular Basis of Inheritance

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

### The DNA Structure
The DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid) is a long polymer of **deoxyribonucleotides**. The length of DNA is defined as the number of **nucleotides** (or a pair of nucleotides referred to as **base pairs**) present in it.
- **Nucleotide composition:** A **pentose sugar** (deoxyribose), a **nitrogenous base** (Purines: **Adenine, Guanine**; Pyrimidines: **Cytosine, Thymine**), and a **phosphate group**.
- **Linkages:** 
  - The nitrogenous base is linked to the pentose sugar through an **N-glycosidic linkage** to form a **nucleoside**.
  - A phosphate group is linked to the 5'-OH of a nucleoside through a **phosphodiester linkage** to form a **nucleotide**.
  - Two nucleotides are linked through a **3'-5' phosphodiester linkage** to form a **dinucleotide**.
- **Double Helix Model (Watson and Crick, 1953):** 
  - It is made of two polynucleotide chains, where the backbone is constituted by **sugar-phosphate**, and the bases project inside.
  - The two chains have **anti-parallel polarity** (5'->3' and 3'->5').
  - The bases in two strands are paired through **hydrogen bonds** (H-bonds) forming base pairs (bp). **Adenine** forms **two H-bonds** with **Thymine** from the opposite strand and vice-versa. **Guanine** is bonded with **Cytosine** with **three H-bonds**. This creates **complementary base pairing**.
  - The pitch of the helix is **3.4 nm**, and there are roughly **10 bp in each turn**. The distance between a bp in a helix is approximately **0.34 nm**.

### Central Dogma
Proposed by **Francis Crick**, it states that the genetic information flows from **DNA -> RNA -> Protein**.
- **Replication:** DNA to DNA.
- **Transcription:** DNA to mRNA.
- **Translation:** mRNA to Protein.
- *Exception:* In some viruses (e.g., Retroviruses), the flow is reverse (**Reverse Transcription**).

### Packaging of DNA Helix
In prokaryotes (e.g., E. coli), DNA is held with some proteins in a region termed as **nucleoid**.
In eukaryotes, there is a set of positively charged, basic proteins called **histones**. They are rich in basic amino acid residues **lysine** and **arginine**. 
- Histones are organized to form a unit of eight molecules called **histone octamer**.
- The negatively charged DNA is wrapped around the positively charged histone octamer to form a structure called **nucleosome**. A typical nucleosome contains **200 bp** of DNA helix.
- Nucleosomes constitute the repeating unit of a structure in the nucleus called **chromatin**.
- **Euchromatin:** Loosely packed, stains light, and is **transcriptionally active**.
- **Heterochromatin:** Densely packed, stains dark, and is **transcriptionally inactive**.

## 2. Step-by-Step Mechanisms

### A. Mechanism of DNA Replication (Semi-conservative)
1. **Origin of Replication:** Replication begins at a specific region on the DNA termed the origin of replication (**ori**).
2. **Unwinding:** The enzyme **DNA helicase** unwinds the DNA double helix by breaking the hydrogen bonds. This creates a Y-shaped structure called a **replication fork**.
3. **Primer Binding:** The enzyme **primase** synthesizes a short RNA primer to initiate synthesis, providing a free 3'-OH group.
4. **Elongation:** The main enzyme, **DNA-dependent DNA polymerase**, catalyzes the polymerization of deoxynucleotides only in one direction, that is **5' -> 3'**. 
   - On one strand (**the template with polarity 3'->5'**), the replication is **continuous** (**Leading strand**).
   - On the other strand (**the template with polarity 5'->3'**), it is **discontinuous** (**Lagging strand**). These short segments are called **Okazaki fragments**.
5. **Ligation:** The discontinuously synthesized Okazaki fragments are later joined by the enzyme **DNA ligase**.

### B. Mechanism of Transcription
Transcription is the process of copying genetic information from one strand of the DNA into RNA. 
The **Transcription Unit** primarily consists of three regions in the DNA: a **Promoter**, the **Structural gene**, and a **Terminator**. 
1. **Initiation:** **DNA-dependent RNA polymerase** binds to the **promoter** region and initiates transcription. It associates transiently with the **initiation factor (sigma - σ)** to alter its specificity.
2. **Elongation:** RNA polymerase unzips the DNA and uses nucleoside triphosphates as substrates to polymerize in a template-dependent fashion following the rule of complementarity (except Thymine is replaced by **Uracil** in RNA). The enzyme facilitates opening of the helix and continues elongation in the **5' -> 3'** direction. 
3. **Termination:** Once the polymerase reaches the **terminator** region, the nascent RNA falls off, and so does the RNA polymerase. This is facilitated by the **termination factor (rho - ρ)**.

*In Eukaryotes:* There are three types of RNA polymerases (I, II, III). The primary transcript (**hnRNA**) contains both **exons** (coding sequences) and **introns** (non-coding). It undergoes:
- **Splicing:** Introns are removed, and exons are joined.
- **Capping:** An unusual nucleotide (**methyl guanosine triphosphate**) is added to the 5'-end.
- **Tailing (Polyadenylation):** Poly(A) tail (200-300 adenylate residues) is added at the 3'-end. Now it is called **mRNA**.

### C. Mechanism of Translation
Translation refers to the process of polymerization of amino acids to form a polypeptide.
1. **Charging of tRNA (Aminoacylation):** Amino acids are activated in the presence of ATP and linked to their cognate tRNA.
2. **Initiation:** The ribosome consists of structural RNAs and about 80 different proteins. In its inactive state, it exists as two subunits (large and small). The small subunit binds to the mRNA at the **start codon (AUG)** that is recognized only by the **initiator tRNA**. 
3. **Elongation:** The large subunit binds, providing two sites (A site and P site) for subsequent amino acids to bind to and thus be close enough to each other for the formation of a **peptide bond**. The ribosome moves from codon to codon along the mRNA (**translocation**).
4. **Termination:** The process continues until a **stop codon (UAA, UAG, or UGA)** is reached. A **release factor** binds to the stop codon, terminating translation and releasing the complete polypeptide from the ribosome.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Subjective Trap (ISC Focus):
- **Forgetting Polarities:** When drawing a replication fork or transcription unit, failure to write the 5' and 3' polarities on BOTH template and newly synthesized strands results in an immediate loss of marks. 
- **Confusing Template and Coding Strands:** In transcription, the strand with 3'->5' polarity acts as the **template strand**. The strand with 5'->3' polarity, which does not code for anything, is called the **coding strand**. This is a naming convention trap. The RNA sequence is identical to the *coding strand* (except U replaces T).

### Objective Trap (NEET/JEE Focus):
- **Bonds per turn:** A turn of DNA is 3.4 nm long with 10 base pairs. Remember that Adenine-Thymine has **2 H-bonds** and Cytosine-Guanine has **3 H-bonds**. Questions often ask for total H-bonds given the composition.
- **Enzyme specifics:** DNA Polymerase always synthesizes in the 5' -> 3' direction. Therefore, it reads the template in the 3' -> 5' direction. Do not mix these up.

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

### Problem 1: Transcription Sequence Translation
**Given:** The sequence of the coding strand of a DNA molecule is `5'-ATGCATGCATGC-3'`. Write the sequence of the mRNA and the corresponding template strand.

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. Identify the given strand: Coding strand = `5'-ATGCATGCATGC-3'`.
2. Find the template strand. The template strand is complementary and anti-parallel to the coding strand.
   - Complementary to A is T, T is A, G is C, C is G.
   - Template strand = `3'-TACGTACGTACG-5'`.
3. Find the mRNA. The mRNA is transcribed from the template strand. It will be complementary and anti-parallel to the template strand (meaning it will have the same sequence and polarity as the coding strand, but with Uracil instead of Thymine).
   - mRNA = `5'-AUGCAUGCAUGC-3'`.
*ISC Marking scheme allocates 1 mark for template strand with polarity, 1 mark for mRNA with polarity.*

**NEET Speed Method:**
mRNA sequence is exactly the same as the coding strand, just replace 'T' with 'U'.
Coding: `5'-A T G C A T G C A T G C-3'`
mRNA: `5'-A U G C A U G C A U G C-3'`
Done in 2 seconds.

### Problem 2: Calculating Length of DNA
**Given:** If the length of E. coli DNA is 1.36 mm, calculate the number of base pairs in E.coli.

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. State the known values:
   - Length of DNA = $1.36 \text{ mm} = 1.36 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}$
   - Distance between two consecutive base pairs = $0.34 \text{ nm} = 0.34 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m}$
2. State the formula:
   - $\text{Total length} = \text{Total base pairs} \times \text{distance between consecutive bps}$
3. Substitute and calculate:
   - $\text{Total base pairs} = \frac{1.36 \times 10^{-3}}{0.34 \times 10^{-9}}$
   - $\text{Total base pairs} = \frac{1.36}{0.34} \times 10^6$
   - $\text{Total base pairs} = 4 \times 10^6 \text{ bp}$

**NEET Speed Method:**
Recognize standard numbers: $1.36 / 0.34 = 4$. $10^{-3} / 10^{-9} = 10^6$. Answer is $4 \times 10^6$ bp.
