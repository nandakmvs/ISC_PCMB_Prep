# 06. Molecular Basis of Inheritance

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

### The DNA Structure
The DNA (Deoxyribonucleic acid) is a long polymer of **deoxyribonucleotides**. The length of DNA is defined as the number of **nucleotides** (or a pair of nucleotides referred to as **base pairs**) present in it.
- **Nucleotide composition:** A **pentose sugar** (deoxyribose), a **nitrogenous base** (Purines: **Adenine, Guanine**; Pyrimidines: **Cytosine, Thymine**), and a **phosphate group**.
- **Linkages:** 
  - The nitrogenous base is linked to the pentose sugar through an **N-glycosidic linkage** to form a **nucleoside**.
  - A phosphate group is linked to the 5'-OH of a nucleoside through a **phosphodiester linkage** to form a **nucleotide**.
  - Two nucleotides are linked through a **3'-5' phosphodiester linkage** to form a **dinucleotide**.
- **Double Helix Model (Watson and Crick, 1953):** 
  - It is made of two polynucleotide chains, where the backbone is constituted by **sugar-phosphate**, and the bases project inside.
  - The two chains have **anti-parallel polarity** (5'->3' and 3'->5').
  - The bases in two strands are paired through **hydrogen bonds** (H-bonds) forming base pairs (bp). **Adenine** forms **two H-bonds** with **Thymine** from the opposite strand and vice-versa. **Guanine** is bonded with **Cytosine** with **three H-bonds**. This creates **complementary base pairing**.
  - The pitch of the helix is **3.4 nm**, and there are roughly **10 bp in each turn**. The distance between a bp in a helix is approximately **0.34 nm**.

### Packaging of DNA Helix
In prokaryotes (e.g., E. coli), DNA is held with some proteins in a region termed as **nucleoid**.
In eukaryotes, there is a set of positively charged, basic proteins called **histones**. They are rich in basic amino acid residues **lysine** and **arginine**. 
- Histones are organized to form a unit of eight molecules called **histone octamer**.
- The negatively charged DNA is wrapped around the positively charged histone octamer to form a structure called **nucleosome**. A typical nucleosome contains **200 bp** of DNA helix.
- Nucleosomes constitute the repeating unit of a structure in the nucleus called **chromatin**.
- **Euchromatin:** Loosely packed, stains light, and is **transcriptionally active**.
- **Heterochromatin:** Densely packed, stains dark, and is **transcriptionally inactive**.

### The Search for Genetic Material
- **Transforming Principle (Frederick Griffith, 1928):** Used *Streptococcus pneumoniae*. He observed a physical change (transformation) of rough (R) non-virulent strain into smooth (S) virulent strain when mixed with heat-killed S strain. He concluded a "transforming principle" was transferred, but the biochemical nature was unknown.
- **Biochemical Characterization (Avery, MacLeod, McCarty, 1944):** Purified biochemicals (proteins, DNA, RNA) from heat-killed S cells. They discovered that **DNA-digesting enzymes (DNases)** inhibited transformation, while proteases and RNases did not. This proved DNA causes transformation.
- **Unequivocal Proof (Hershey and Chase, 1952):** Used bacteriophages (viruses that infect bacteria). They grew viruses on radioactive phosphorus (${}^{32}P$) to label DNA, and radioactive sulfur (${}^{35}S$) to label proteins. Upon infecting *E. coli*, only radioactive phosphorus was found inside the bacteria, proving unequivocally that **DNA is the genetic material**.

### Central Dogma & Genetic Code
Proposed by **Francis Crick**, it states that the genetic information flows from **DNA -> RNA -> Protein**.
- **Genetic Code:** The sequence of nucleotides in mRNA that dictates the amino acid sequence. 
  - **Triplet Code:** 3 bases code for 1 amino acid (64 codons total; 61 code for amino acids, 3 are **stop codons**: UAA, UAG, UGA).
  - **Unambiguous & Specific:** One codon codes for only one amino acid.
  - **Degenerate:** Some amino acids are coded by more than one codon.
  - **Universal:** UUU codes for Phenylalanine in bacteria as well as humans (with minor mitochondrial exceptions).
  - **Start Codon:** **AUG** has dual functions—it codes for Methionine (met) and acts as an initiator codon.

### Mutations
- **Point Mutation:** Change of a single base pair. Example: Sickle cell anemia (GAG to GUG in beta-globin chain).
- **Frameshift Mutation:** Insertion or deletion of one or two bases, changing the reading frame from the point of insertion/deletion.

### Regulation of Gene Expression
Expression can be controlled at various levels in eukaryotes (Transcriptional, Processing/Splicing, Transport to cytoplasm, Translational). In prokaryotes, it is predominantly regulated at the **transcriptional initiation** level.
- **Operon Concept (Jacob and Monod):** A polycistronic structural gene regulated by a common promoter and regulatory genes. Examples: *lac* operon, *trp* operon.

### Human Genome Project (HGP) & DNA Fingerprinting
- **HGP Goals:** Sequence the entire 3 billion base pairs of human DNA, store information in databases, and address ethical, legal, and social issues (ELSI).
- **Salient Features of Human Genome:** The human genome contains 3164.7 million bp. Average gene consists of 3000 bases. Largest known human gene is **dystrophin**. Less than 2% codes for proteins.
- **DNA Fingerprinting (Alec Jeffreys):** A technique to identify variations in individuals at the DNA level. It relies on **Variable Number of Tandem Repeats (VNTRs)**, which show a very high degree of **polymorphism**.

## 2. Step-by-Step Mechanisms

### A. The Hershey-Chase Experiment (1952)
1. **Infection:** Bacteriophages labeled with either ${}^{32}P$ (DNA) or ${}^{35}S$ (Protein) were allowed to attach to and infect *E. coli* bacteria.
2. **Blending:** The viral coats were removed from the bacterial surface by spinning them in a blender.
3. **Centrifugation:** The virus particles were separated from the bacteria. Bacteria, being heavier, formed a pellet at the bottom, while lighter viral coats remained in the supernatant.
4. **Observation:** Bacteria infected with ${}^{32}P$ viruses were radioactive (pellet). Bacteria infected with ${}^{35}S$ were not radioactive, but the supernatant was. Conclusion: DNA is the genetic material.

### B. Meselson-Stahl Experiment (Semi-Conservative Replication Proof, 1958)
1. **Heavy Isotope Labeling:** Grew *E. coli* in a medium containing ${}^{15}NH_4Cl$ (heavy nitrogen) for many generations until all DNA was heavy (${}^{15}N-{}^{15}N$).
2. **Transfer:** Transferred the cells to a normal ${}^{14}NH_4Cl$ medium.
3. **Sampling & Centrifugation:** Extracted DNA at specific time intervals (e.g., 20 mins for one *E. coli* generation) and centrifuged it in a **Cesium Chloride (CsCl) density gradient**.
4. **Results:** After generation 1 (20 mins), DNA was **hybrid/intermediate** density (${}^{14}N-{}^{15}N$). After generation 2 (40 mins), there was equal amounts of hybrid DNA and light DNA (${}^{14}N-{}^{14}N$). This proved semi-conservative replication.

### C. Mechanism of DNA Replication
1. **Origin of Replication:** Replication begins at a specific region on the DNA termed the origin of replication (**ori**).
2. **Unwinding:** The enzyme **DNA helicase** unwinds the DNA double helix by breaking the hydrogen bonds. This creates a Y-shaped structure called a **replication fork**.
3. **Primer Binding:** The enzyme **primase** synthesizes a short RNA primer to initiate synthesis, providing a free 3'-OH group.
4. **Elongation:** The main enzyme, **DNA-dependent DNA polymerase**, catalyzes the polymerization of deoxynucleotides only in one direction, that is **5' -> 3'**. 
   - On one strand (**the template with polarity 3'->5'**), the replication is **continuous** (**Leading strand**).
   - On the other strand (**the template with polarity 5'->3'**), it is **discontinuous** (**Lagging strand**). These short segments are called **Okazaki fragments**.
5. **Ligation:** The discontinuously synthesized Okazaki fragments are joined by **DNA ligase**.

### D. Mechanism of Transcription
1. **Initiation:** **DNA-dependent RNA polymerase** binds to the **promoter** region. It associates with the **initiation factor (sigma - σ)**.
2. **Elongation:** RNA polymerase uses nucleoside triphosphates to polymerize in a template-dependent fashion following complementarity (Uracil replaces Thymine). Elongation occurs in the **5' -> 3'** direction. 
3. **Termination:** Upon reaching the **terminator** region, the polymerase associates with the **termination factor (rho - ρ)**, falling off and releasing the nascent RNA.

### E. Mechanism of Translation
1. **Charging of tRNA (Aminoacylation):** Amino acids are activated with ATP and linked to their cognate tRNA.
2. **Initiation:** The small ribosomal subunit binds to mRNA at the **start codon (AUG)**. The initiator tRNA brings Methionine. 
3. **Elongation:** The large subunit binds. Subsequent tRNAs enter the A site. A **peptide bond** forms between amino acids, catalyzed by the ribosome (ribozyme). The ribosome moves (**translocates**) along the mRNA.
4. **Termination:** A **stop codon (UAA, UAG, or UGA)** is reached. A **release factor** binds to it, terminating translation.

### F. The Lac Operon Mechanism
The *lac* operon controls lactose metabolism in *E. coli*. It consists of a regulator gene (*i* gene) and three structural genes (*z, y, a*).
- **In the Absence of Inducer (Lactose):** The *i* gene synthesizes the **repressor protein** continuously (constitutive). The repressor binds to the **operator** region, preventing RNA polymerase from transcribing the structural genes. (Operon is OFF).
- **In the Presence of Inducer (Lactose/Allolactose):** The inducer binds to the repressor, causing a conformational change that inactivates it. The inactive repressor cannot bind to the operator. RNA polymerase accesses the promoter and transcribes the *z* (beta-galactosidase), *y* (permease), and *a* (transacetylase) genes. (Operon is ON). This is **negative regulation**.

### G. Mechanism of DNA Fingerprinting
1. **Isolation** of DNA.
2. **Digestion** of DNA by restriction endonucleases.
3. **Separation** of DNA fragments by gel electrophoresis.
4. **Southern Blotting:** Transferring separated DNA fragments to synthetic membranes (nitrocellulose or nylon).
5. **Hybridization** using labeled VNTR probes.
6. **Autoradiography:** Detection of hybridized DNA fragments by exposure to X-ray film, producing a pattern of bands unique to the individual.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Subjective Trap (ISC Focus):
- **Forgetting Polarities:** When drawing a replication fork or transcription unit, failure to write the 5' and 3' polarities on BOTH template and newly synthesized strands results in an immediate loss of marks. 
- **Lac Operon Terminology:** Do not confuse the *i* gene with the word "inducer". The *i* gene codes for the inhibitor/repressor. The inducer is lactose.
- **Griffith vs. Avery:** If a question asks "Who proved DNA is the genetic material biochemically?", the answer is Avery, MacLeod, and McCarty. If it asks "unequivocally," the answer is Hershey and Chase. Griffith only discovered the *principle*.

### Objective Trap (NEET/JEE Focus):
- **Meselson-Stahl Math:** If E. coli grows for 80 minutes (4 generations), the ratio of hybrid to light DNA is not 50/50. Out of 16 DNA molecules, only 2 will be hybrid. The ratio of Hybrid:Light is 2:14 or 1:7.
- **Bonds per turn:** A turn of DNA is 3.4 nm long with 10 base pairs. Remember that Adenine-Thymine has **2 H-bonds** and Cytosine-Guanine has **3 H-bonds**.
- **Stop Codons:** UAA (Ochre), UAG (Amber), UGA (Opal). They do *not* code for any amino acids.

### Distinction Tables

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

### Problem 1: Meselson-Stahl Ratio Calculation
**Given:** E. coli fully labeled with ${}^{15}N$ is allowed to grow in ${}^{14}N$ medium. What will be the percentage of hybrid and light DNA after 60 minutes? (Generation time = 20 mins).

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. State the number of generations: 60 mins / 20 mins = 3 generations.
2. Initial State (Gen 0): 1 molecule, 100% Heavy (${}^{15}N-{}^{15}N$).
3. After Gen 1 (20 mins): 2 molecules, both Hybrid (${}^{14}N-{}^{15}N$).
4. After Gen 2 (40 mins): 4 molecules total. 2 Hybrid, 2 Light (${}^{14}N-{}^{14}N$).
5. After Gen 3 (60 mins): 8 molecules total. The 2 Hybrid molecules remain 2 (they just pass their single ${}^{15}N$ strand to two new molecules). The rest (8 - 2 = 6) are Light.
6. Calculate percentage: 
   - Hybrid = $(2/8) \times 100 = 25\%$
   - Light = $(6/8) \times 100 = 75\%$

**NEET Speed Method:**
Rule: The number of hybrid molecules is ALWAYS 2 for any generation after the first transfer. 
Total molecules after $n$ generations = $2^n$. 
For 3 generations (60 mins), total = $2^3 = 8$. 
Hybrid = 2. Light = $8 - 2 = 6$. Ratio is 2:6 or 1:3 (25% to 75%).

### Problem 2: Transcription Sequence Translation
**Given:** The sequence of the coding strand of a DNA molecule is `5'-ATGCATGCATGC-3'`. Write the sequence of the mRNA and the corresponding template strand.

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. Identify the given strand: Coding strand = `5'-ATGCATGCATGC-3'`.
2. Find the template strand. It is complementary and anti-parallel to the coding strand.
   - Template strand = `3'-TACGTACGTACG-5'`.
3. Find the mRNA. It is transcribed from the template strand, complementary and anti-parallel to it.
   - mRNA = `5'-AUGCAUGCAUGC-3'`.

**NEET Speed Method:**
mRNA sequence is exactly the same as the coding strand, just replace 'T' with 'U'.
Coding: `5'-A T G C A T G C A T G C-3'`
mRNA: `5'-A U G C A U G C A U G C-3'`

### Problem 3: Lac Operon Scenarios
**Given:** A mutation occurs in the *i* gene of the lac operon, producing a repressor protein that cannot bind to lactose. What is the status of the operon in the presence of lactose?

**ISC Step-by-Step Method:**
1. Identify the function of the mutated protein: The repressor cannot bind to the inducer (lactose).
2. Consequence: The repressor remains active and bound to the operator region permanently.
3. Conclusion: RNA polymerase cannot access the promoter. The structural genes (*z, y, a*) will not be transcribed. The operon is permanently turned OFF, even in the presence of lactose.
