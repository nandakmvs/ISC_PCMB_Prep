# Chapter 9: Ray Optics and Optical Instruments

## 9.1 Reflection of Light & Spherical Mirrors

### Key Concepts
- **Laws of Reflection**: Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection; incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane.
- **Spherical Mirrors**: Concave (converging) and Convex (diverging).
- **Sign Convention**: Cartesian sign convention is strictly followed for all numericals.
  - Pole is the origin.
  - Direction of incident light is positive.
- **Mirror Formula**: $1/f = 1/v + 1/u$
- **Magnification**: $m = -v/u = h_i/h_o$

### Derivations (SJBHC Focus)
1. **Relation between $f$ and $R$**: $f = R/2$ for spherical mirrors.
   *Practice drawing neat ray diagrams for this derivation. Ensure angles are clearly marked.*
2. **Mirror Formula Derivation**: Using similar triangles for a concave mirror forming a real image. *Crucial for board exams.*

### Competency Based Education (CBE) - Assertion & Reason
**Assertion (A):** The focal length of a convex mirror is always positive.
**Reason (R):** The center of curvature of a convex mirror lies behind the mirror, in the direction of incident light.
*Answer:* Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

### Numerical Focus
- Finding image position and nature for objects placed at various positions (especially between $F$ and $P$ for concave mirrors).
- Multi-concept problems combining reflection and refraction.

## 9.2 Refraction of Light & Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

### Key Concepts
- **Snell's Law**: $\mu = \sin(i)/\sin(r) = v_1/v_2$. 
- **Refractive Index**: Absolute and relative. Principle of reversibility.
- **Real and Apparent Depth**: Shift = $t(1 - 1/\mu)$.
- **Total Internal Reflection (TIR)**: Light travels from denser to rarer medium and $i > i_c$ (critical angle).
  - Relation: $\mu = 1/\sin(i_c)$

### Derivations (SJBHC Focus)
1. **Apparent Shift**: Geometrical derivation of apparent depth and shift.
2. **Critical Angle Relation**: Simple proof using Snell's Law.

### Competency Based Education (CBE) - Assertion & Reason
**Assertion (A):** Diamonds sparkle brilliantly compared to glass cut to the same shape.
**Reason (R):** The critical angle for diamond is very small, leading to multiple total internal reflections.
*Answer:* Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

### Numerical Focus
- Calculation of apparent shift for composite slabs (multiple media).
- Finding the field of view of a fish underwater (radius of the circular patch of light).

## 9.3 Refraction at Spherical Surfaces and Lenses

### Key Concepts
- **Refraction at a Convex/Concave Surface**: $\frac{\mu_2}{v} - \frac{\mu_1}{u} = \frac{\mu_2 - \mu_1}{R}$
- **Lenses**: Convex and Concave.
- **Lens Maker's Formula**: $\frac{1}{f} = (\mu - 1)\left(\frac{1}{R_1} - \frac{1}{R_2}\right)$
- **Lens Formula**: $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}$
- **Power of a Lens**: $P = \frac{1}{f}$ (in meters), Unit: Diopter (D).
- **Combination of Lenses**: $\frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2}$

### Derivations (SJBHC Focus)
1. **Refraction at Spherical Surface**: *Highly tested.* Trace rays carefully, apply Snell's Law and small angle approximations.
2. **Lens Maker's Formula**: Derived by applying the spherical surface formula twice. *Critical derivation.*
3. **Equivalent Focal Length**: For two thin lenses in contact.

### Competency Based Education (CBE) - Assertion & Reason
**Assertion (A):** A biconvex lens of glass ($\mu = 1.5$) behaves as a diverging lens when immersed in carbon disulfide ($\mu = 1.6$).
**Reason (R):** The focal length of a lens changes sign when the surrounding medium has a higher refractive index than the lens material.
*Answer:* Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

### Numerical Focus
- Lens Maker's Formula problems involving cutting lenses (vertically/horizontally) and immersing them in liquids.
- Two-lens systems separated by a distance (finding the final image).

## 9.4 Refraction through a Prism & Dispersion

### Key Concepts
- **Prism Formula**: $\mu = \frac{\sin((A + \delta_m)/2)}{\sin(A/2)}$
- **Deviation**: $\delta = i + e - A$. At minimum deviation, $i = e$ and $r_1 = r_2$.
- **Dispersion**: Splitting of white light into constituent colors.
- **Angular Dispersion & Dispersive Power**: $\omega = \frac{\delta_V - \delta_R}{\delta_Y}$.

### Derivations (SJBHC Focus)
1. **Prism Equation**: Derivation of $A + \delta = i + e$.
2. **Refractive Index of Prism**: Derivation of the formula involving $\delta_m$ and $A$.

### Competency Based Education (CBE) - Assertion & Reason
**Assertion (A):** Red light deviates the least when passing through a prism.
**Reason (R):** The refractive index of glass is minimum for red light.
*Answer:* Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

### Numerical Focus
- Calculating angle of minimum deviation for prisms of different refractive indices.
- Grazing emergence problems (finding the maximum angle of incidence for transmission).
- Small angle prisms ($\delta = A(\mu - 1)$).

## 9.5 Optical Instruments

### Key Concepts
- **Simple Microscope**: Magnifying power $M = 1 + D/f$ (image at D) or $M = D/f$ (image at infinity).
- **Compound Microscope**: Objective and eyepiece. $M = M_o \times M_e = (v_o/u_o)(1 + D/f_e)$.
  - Tube length $L = v_o + u_e$.
- **Astronomical Telescope**: Objective (large aperture/focal length) and eyepiece (small aperture/focal length).
  - Normal adjustment: $M = f_o/f_e$, $L = f_o + f_e$.
- **Resolving Power**: Of microscope ($2\mu\sin\theta / \lambda$) and telescope ($a / (1.22\lambda)$).

### Derivations (SJBHC Focus)
1. **Magnifying Power of Compound Microscope**: Draw clear, large ray diagrams showing both intermediate and final images.
2. **Magnifying Power of Astronomical Telescope**: Derivation for both normal adjustment and near point.

### Competency Based Education (CBE) - Assertion & Reason
**Assertion (A):** The objective lens of an astronomical telescope has a large aperture.
**Reason (R):** A large aperture increases the light-gathering power and resolving power of the telescope.
*Answer:* Both A and R are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.

### Numerical Focus
- Calculating magnifying power and tube length for compound microscopes and telescopes given the focal lengths of the lenses.
- Adjustments needed to change from near point to normal adjustment viewing.
