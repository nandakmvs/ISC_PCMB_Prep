**Q27: Briefly describe the replication cycle of a retrovirus (like HIV) inside a human macrophage.**
**Perfect ISC Answer:**
1. **Entry:** The virus enters the macrophage and its RNA genome is released into the cytoplasm.
2. **Reverse Transcription:** The viral enzyme reverse transcriptase synthesizes viral DNA from the viral RNA.
3. **Integration:** This viral DNA enters the host nucleus and integrates into the host cell's DNA via the enzyme integrase.
4. **Transcription & Translation:** The infected cell's machinery produces new viral RNA and viral proteins.
5. **Budding (The missing step!):** The new viral RNA and proteins assemble to form new HIV particles, which **bud off** from the macrophage. The macrophage survives and acts like an **"HIV factory"**, continuing to produce viruses.
