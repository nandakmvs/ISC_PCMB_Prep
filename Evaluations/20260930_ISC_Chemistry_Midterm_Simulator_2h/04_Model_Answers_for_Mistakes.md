### 1. IUPAC Naming of Coordination Compounds
**Mistake:** Dropping the oxidation state and misspelling ligands.
**Correction:** 
The rules for naming coordination compounds are strict:
1. **Cation first, Anion second.** (Just like Sodium chloride).
2. **Inside the coordination sphere:** Name the ligands first in *alphabetical order*, then name the central metal.
3. **Ligand names:** Anionic ligands end in '-o' (e.g. $Cl^-$ is *chlorido*, $Br^-$ is *bromido*). Neutral ligands have specific names ($NH_3$ is **ammine** - note the double 'm', $H_2O$ is *aqua*).
4. **Oxidation State:** The oxidation state of the metal MUST be written in Roman numerals in parentheses immediately after the metal name, with no space.

**Example 1:** $[Co(NH_3)_6]Cl_3$
- Ligand: $NH_3$ (hexaammine)
- Metal: Cobalt
- Oxidation state calculation: $x + 6(0) = +3 \implies x = +3$
- Anion: Chloride
- **Correct Name:** hexaamminecobalt(III) chloride

**Example 2:** $[Co(NH_3)_5(SO_4)]Br$
- Wait, the student wrote $[Co(NH_3)_5(SO_4)]Br$. Let's assume the formula was $[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4$ based on her text.
- If it's $[Co(NH_3)_5Br]SO_4$:
- Ligands: ammine and bromido. Alphabetical: pentaammine, then bromido.
- Metal: Cobalt
- Oxidation state: $x + 5(0) + (-1) = +2$ (since $SO_4$ is -2 outside). Wait, if $SO_4$ is outside, the complex is $+2$. So $x - 1 = +2 \implies x = +3$.
- **Correct Name:** pentaamminebromidocobalt(III) sulfate

### 2. Solutions Numericals and van't Hoff Factor ($i$)
**Mistake:** Not explicitly stating the van't Hoff factor.
**Correction:** 
Always write the generalized colligative property formula:
$\Delta T_f = i \cdot K_f \cdot m$
If the solute is a non-electrolyte (does not dissociate or associate, e.g., glucose, urea, sucrose), explicitly write:
*Since the solute is a non-electrolyte, the van't Hoff factor $i = 1$.*
Then proceed with the calculation. This secures you against losing half a mark for missing steps.
