**(Questions 16 to 20: Assertion-Reasoning)**
*Choose the correct option from the following:*
*(a) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.*
*(b) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.*
*(c) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.*
*(d) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.*

**16. Assertion (A):** Every differentiable function is continuous, but the converse is not true.
**Reason (R):** The function $f(x) = |x|$ is continuous at $x=0$ but not differentiable at $x=0$.

**17. Assertion (A):** A singular matrix has no inverse.
**Reason (R):** For a singular matrix $A$, $|A| = 0$, and the formula for inverse involves dividing by $|A|$.

**18. Assertion (A):** If $l, m, n$ are the direction cosines of a vector, then $l^2 + m^2 + n^2 = 1$.
**Reason (R):** The direction ratios of a vector are strictly proportional to its direction cosines.

**19. Assertion (A):** For independent events $A$ and $B$, $P(A \cap B) = P(A) + P(B)$.
**Reason (R):** The probability of the intersection of two independent events is the product of their individual probabilities.

**20. Assertion (A):** The domain of $\sec^{-1} x$ is $\mathbb{R} - (-1, 1)$.
**Reason (R):** The secant function can take any real value between -1 and 1.

*Answer both case studies. Each sub-part carries 2 marks.*

**21. Case Study 1: Aerospace Navigation (Vectors)**
An air traffic controller is tracking two drones. Drone A is located at coordinates $P(2, -1, 3)$ and Drone B is located at $Q(1, 1, 1)$. The control tower is at the origin $O(0,0,0)$. 
(i) Find the position vectors $\vec{p}$ and $\vec{q}$ of the drones, and calculate the distance between Drone A and Drone B (magnitude of vector $\vec{PQ}$). (2 Marks)
(ii) The controller needs to ensure the flight paths from the tower are sufficiently separated. Find the cosine of the angle between the vectors $\vec{OP}$ and $\vec{OQ}$. (2 Marks)

**22. Case Study 2: Supply Chain Management (Matrices)**
A manufacturing plant uses a combination of three machines ($M_1, M_2, M_3$) to produce three different car components ($C_1, C_2, C_3$). The plant manager has recorded the following time constraints (in hours) to produce a batch of each component:
- To produce a batch of $C_1$, it takes 1 hour on $M_1$, 1 hour on $M_2$, and 1 hour on $M_3$.
- To produce a batch of $C_2$, it takes 1 hour on $M_1$, 2 hours on $M_2$, and 3 hours on $M_3$.
- To produce a batch of $C_3$, it takes 2 hours on $M_1$, 3 hours on $M_2$, and 1 hour on $M_3$.
The total available daily hours for $M_1, M_2,$ and $M_3$ are 10, 16, and 14 hours respectively.

(i) Formulate this scenario as a system of linear equations in the form $AX = B$, explicitly writing out the matrices $A$, $X$, and $B$. (2 Marks)
(ii) Find the determinant of matrix $A$ to verify if a unique solution exists for the number of batches that can be produced. (2 Marks)
