**(Questions 16 to 20: Assertion-Reasoning)**
*Choose the correct option from the following:*
*(a) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, and R is the correct explanation of A.*
*(b) Both Assertion (A) and Reason (R) are true, but R is NOT the correct explanation of A.*
*(c) Assertion (A) is true but Reason (R) is false.*
*(d) Assertion (A) is false but Reason (R) is true.*

**16. Assertion (A):** The matrix $A = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ -2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$ is a skew-symmetric matrix.
**Reason (R):** A square matrix $A$ is said to be skew-symmetric if $A^T = -A$.

**17. Assertion (A):** The relation $R$ on the set $\mathbb{Z}$ of integers defined by $R = \{(x,y) : x - y \text{ is an integer}\}$ is an equivalence relation.
**Reason (R):** The relation is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

**18. Assertion (A):** The vectors $\hat{i} + \hat{j}$ and $\hat{i} - \hat{j}$ are orthogonal (perpendicular) to each other.
**Reason (R):** The cross product of two non-zero perpendicular vectors is zero.

**19. Assertion (A):** The derivative of $f(x) = e^x$ with respect to $g(x) = \sqrt{x}$ at $x=1$ is $2e$.
**Reason (R):** Derivative of a function with respect to another function is given by $\frac{df/dx}{dg/dx}$.

**20. Assertion (A):** If events $A$ and $B$ are mutually exclusive, then $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \cdot P(B)$.
**Reason (R):** Mutually exclusive events cannot occur simultaneously.

## SECTION B: Case Studies / Source-Based Questions (8 Marks)
*Answer both case studies. Each sub-part carries 2 marks.*

**21. Case Study 1: Medical Diagnostics (Bayes' Theorem)**
A reliable diagnostic test for a newly discovered virus is developed. 
- The test is 95% effective in detecting the disease when it is present (True Positive).
- However, it also yields a false positive result for 2% of the healthy people tested. 
- Epidemiological data shows that only 1% of the total population actually has the virus.
A patient goes to the clinic, takes the test, and the result comes back POSITIVE.

(i) Draw a probability tree diagram or explicitly state $P(E_1), P(E_2), P(A|E_1),$ and $P(A|E_2)$ where $A$ is the event of testing positive. (2 Marks)
(ii) Using Bayes' Theorem, calculate the exact probability that the patient *actually* has the virus given that they tested positive. (2 Marks)

**22. Case Study 2: Industrial Optimization (Linear Programming)**
A local carpenter manufactures two types of products: Bookshelves ($X$) and Tables ($Y$). 
- Each Bookshelf requires 3 hours of machine time and 1 hour of manual finishing time.
- Each Table requires 2 hours of machine time and 2 hours of manual finishing time.
- The carpenter has a maximum of 18 hours of machine time and 10 hours of manual finishing time available per day.
- He makes a profit of Rs. 400 on each Bookshelf and Rs. 500 on each Table.

(i) Formulate the above scenario as a Linear Programming Problem (LPP) by writing down the objective function and all necessary constraints. (2 Marks)
(ii) Graph the feasible region on a piece of paper, identify the corner points, and determine the number of Bookshelves and Tables he should produce to maximize profit. What is the maximum profit? (2 Marks)
