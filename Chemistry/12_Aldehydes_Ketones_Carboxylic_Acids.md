# Chapter 12: Aldehydes, Ketones, and Carboxylic Acids

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

These contain the carbonyl group (>C=O). 
- **Aldehydes**: Carbonyl group is attached to at least one hydrogen atom (except formaldehyde). Functional group: $-CHO$.
- **Ketones**: Carbonyl group is attached to two carbon atoms. Functional group: $>C=O$.
- **Carboxylic Acids**: Carbonyl group is attached to a hydroxyl group. Functional group: $-COOH$.

### Preparation of Aldehydes and Ketones
1. **Ozonolysis of Alkenes**: Cleaves C=C double bond, adds =O to each carbon (Reductive with $Zn/H_2O$).
2. **Hydration of Alkynes**: Using dil. $H_2SO_4$ and $HgSO_4$ at 333 K. (Ethyne gives acetaldehyde, others give ketones).
3. **Rosenmund Reduction**: Acid chloride to aldehyde using $H_2 / Pd-BaSO_4$. ($BaSO_4$ acts as a catalytic poison to prevent further reduction to alcohol).
4. **Stephen Reaction**: Nitriles to aldehydes using $SnCl_2 / HCl$ followed by hydrolysis ($H_3O^+$).
5. **Etard Reaction**: Toluene to Benzaldehyde using Chromyl chloride ($CrO_2Cl_2$).
6. **Gattermann-Koch Reaction**: Benzene to Benzaldehyde using $CO, HCl$, anhy. $AlCl_3$.

### Chemical Properties: Nucleophilic Addition
The carbonyl carbon is $sp^2$ hybridized and electrophilic (due to electronegative oxygen). Nucleophiles attack the carbon perpendicularly to the plane, forming a tetrahedral $sp^3$ alkoxide intermediate.
- **Reactivity**: Aldehydes are **more reactive** than ketones due to (a) **Steric factors** (ketones have two bulky alkyl groups) and (b) **Electronic factors** (alkyl groups are electron-donating $+I$ effect, reducing the electrophilicity of carbonyl carbon).
- **Addition of HCN**: Forms Cyanohydrins.
- **Addition of $NaHSO_3$**: Forms bisulfite adduct (white crystalline solid). Used for purification of aldehydes.
- **Addition of Grignard Reagent ($RMgX$)**: Formaldehyde $\to 1^\circ$ alcohol. Other aldehydes $\to 2^\circ$ alcohol. Ketones $\to 3^\circ$ alcohol.

### Oxidation and Reduction
- **Oxidation**: 
  - Aldehydes are easily oxidized to carboxylic acids (Tollens' reagent, Fehling's solution).
  - Ketones are oxidized only under vigorous conditions (cleavage of C-C bonds, Popoff's rule).
- **Reduction to Alcohols**: Using $LiAlH_4$ or $NaBH_4$.
- **Reduction to Alkanes**:
  - **Clemmensen Reduction**: $Zn(Hg)$ and conc. $HCl$.
  - **Wolff-Kishner Reduction**: Hydrazine ($NH_2NH_2$) followed by heating with KOH in ethylene glycol.

### Important Name Reactions (Carbonyls)
1. **Aldol Condensation**: Aldehydes/Ketones having **$\alpha$-hydrogen** react with dilute alkali to form $\beta$-hydroxy aldehydes (aldol) or ketones (ketol). Upon heating, they lose water to form $\alpha,\beta$-unsaturated carbonyls.
2. **Cannizzaro Reaction**: Aldehydes having **NO $\alpha$-hydrogen** (e.g., $HCHO, C_6H_5CHO$) undergo self-oxidation-reduction (disproportionation) with conc. alkali to yield an alcohol and a salt of a carboxylic acid.
3. **Haloform (Iodoform) Reaction**: Compounds having $CH_3CO-$ group (or $CH_3CH(OH)-$ which oxidizes to it) react with $NaOI$ ($I_2+NaOH$) to give yellow precipitate of Iodoform ($CHI_3$).

### Carboxylic Acids
- **Acidic Strength**: Depends on the stability of the carboxylate ion. Electron Withdrawing Groups (EWG, $-I$ effect) stabilize the ion $\implies$ **Increase acidity**. Electron Donating Groups (EDG, $+I$ effect) destabilize $\implies$ **Decrease acidity**.
  Order: $CF_3COOH > CCl_3COOH > CHCl_2COOH > CH_2ClCOOH > CH_3COOH$.
- **HVZ (Hell-Volhard-Zelinsky) Reaction**: Carboxylic acids with $\alpha$-hydrogen react with $X_2$ / Red P to give $\alpha$-halocarboxylic acids.

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### Mechanism of Nucleophilic Addition to Carbonyl Group
1. **Attack**: The nucleophile ($Nu^-$) attacks the electrophilic carbonyl carbon at an angle of $\approx 109^\circ$ to the $C-O$ double bond.
2. **Intermediate**: The $\pi$-electron cloud transfers to the oxygen atom. The carbon hybridization changes from $sp^2$ (planar) to $sp^3$ (tetrahedral), forming an alkoxide ion intermediate.
3. **Protonation**: The alkoxide ion captures a proton from the reaction medium to yield the neutral addition product.

## 4. "Avoid the Trap" & Distinction Tables

### Chemical Tests (Crucial for ISC)
| Test | Reagent | Reacts with | Observation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tollens' Test** | Ammoniacal $AgNO_3$ | **All Aldehydes** (Aliphatic & Aromatic) | Silver mirror |
| **Fehling's Test** | Fehling A (CuSO4) + B (Rochelle salt) | **Aliphatic Aldehydes only** | Red ppt of $Cu_2O$ |
| **Iodoform Test** | $I_2$ + NaOH | Methyl ketones, Acetaldehyde, Ethanol, 2-ols | Yellow ppt ($CHI_3$) |
| **NaHCO3 Test** | $NaHCO_3$ | **Carboxylic Acids** | Brisk effervescence ($CO_2$) |

### ⚠️ ISC Traps & Subjective Pitfalls
- **Trap**: Using Fehling's test to distinguish benzaldehyde from acetophenone.
  - *Correction*: Benzaldehyde does **NOT** respond to Fehling's test! Use Tollens' test instead.
- **Trap**: Applying Cannizzaro to acetaldehyde.
  - *Correction*: Acetaldehyde ($CH_3CHO$) has $\alpha$-hydrogens. It will undergo Aldol condensation, not Cannizzaro.

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

### Q1. ISC Subjective Problem: Chemical Distinctions
**Give one chemical test to distinguish between Acetaldehyde and Benzaldehyde.**

**ISC Method:**
1. **Test Name**: Fehling's Test.
2. **Procedure**: Add Fehling's solution (mixture of Fehling A and B) to both compounds and heat.
3. **Observation for Acetaldehyde**: Acetaldehyde, being an aliphatic aldehyde, reduces Fehling's solution to give a **brick-red precipitate** of cuprous oxide ($Cu_2O$).
4. **Observation for Benzaldehyde**: Benzaldehyde, being an aromatic aldehyde, does **not** respond and no red precipitate is formed.
5. **Equation**: $CH_3CHO + 2Cu^{2+} + 5OH^- \xrightarrow{\Delta} CH_3COO^- + Cu_2O\downarrow \text{ (red)} + 3H_2O$.

### Q2. ISC Step-by-Step Problem: Road Map Sequence
**An organic compound (A) with molecular formula $C_8H_8O$ forms an orange-red precipitate with 2,4-DNP reagent and gives yellow precipitate on heating with iodine in the presence of NaOH. It neither reduces Tollens' or Fehling's reagent, nor does it decolorize bromine water. On drastic oxidation with chromic acid, it gives a carboxylic acid (B) having molecular formula $C_7H_6O_2$. Identify (A) and (B).**

**ISC Method:**
1. (A) gives 2,4-DNP test $\implies$ It is a carbonyl compound (aldehyde or ketone).
2. (A) does not reduce Tollens'/Fehling's $\implies$ It is a **ketone**.
3. (A) gives positive iodoform test $\implies$ It is a **methyl ketone** ($CH_3-CO-$ group).
4. Formula $C_8H_8O$, contains benzene ring (implied by high degree of unsaturation and formula). Hence, it must be $C_6H_5-CO-CH_3$ (**Acetophenone**).
5. Drastic oxidation of Acetophenone cleaves the terminal methyl group to yield **Benzoic acid** ($C_6H_5COOH$), which matches formula $C_7H_6O_2$.
6. **Conclusion**: (A) is Acetophenone. (B) is Benzoic acid.
*(Always write the structural formulas for A and B in the final answer).*
