# Chapter 10: Haloalkanes and Haloarenes

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

These are compounds derived by the replacement of hydrogen atom(s) in aliphatic or aromatic hydrocarbons by halogen atom(s).
- **Haloalkanes (Alkyl halides)**: Halogen attached to an $sp^3$ hybridized carbon.
- **Haloarenes (Aryl halides)**: Halogen attached to an $sp^2$ hybridized carbon of an aromatic ring.
- **Allylic halides**: Halogen attached to an $sp^3$ carbon adjacent to a C=C double bond.
- **Benzylic halides**: Halogen attached to an $sp^3$ carbon attached to a benzene ring.
- **Vinylic halides**: Halogen attached directly to an $sp^2$ carbon of a C=C double bond.

### Nature of C-X Bond
The carbon-halogen bond is polarized ($C^{\delta+} - X^{\delta-}$) due to the higher electronegativity of halogens.
- Bond length increases from C-F to C-I.
- Bond enthalpy decreases from C-F to C-I.
- Dipole moment order: $CH_3Cl > CH_3F > CH_3Br > CH_3I$. (Anomaly due to very small size of F).

### Methods of Preparation (Haloalkanes)
1. **From Alcohols**:
   - $ROH + SOCl_2 \rightarrow RCl + SO_2\uparrow + HCl\uparrow$ (**Darzen's process** - preferred because byproducts are escapable gases).
   - $ROH + PCl_5 \rightarrow RCl + POCl_3 + HCl$
   - $3ROH + PCl_3 \rightarrow 3RCl + H_3PO_3$
2. **From Hydrocarbons**:
   - Free radical halogenation of alkanes (gives mixtures).
   - Electrophilic addition of HX to alkenes (**Markovnikov's rule**).
   - Anti-Markovnikov addition (Peroxide effect / **Kharasch effect**) - only for **HBr**.

### Methods of Preparation (Haloarenes)
1. **Electrophilic Substitution**: Direct halogenation of benzene with $X_2$ in presence of Lewis acid ($FeX_3$, $AlCl_3$).
2. **Sandmeyer's Reaction**: $ArN_2^+X^-$ (Diazonium salt) $+ Cu_2X_2 \rightarrow Ar-X + N_2$.
3. **Gattermann Reaction**: $ArN_2^+X^- + Cu/HX \rightarrow Ar-X + N_2$.

### Chemical Properties of Haloalkanes
1. **Nucleophilic Substitution ($S_N$ reactions)**: See Mechanism section.
2. **Elimination Reactions (Dehydrohalogenation)**:
   - Heating with **alcoholic KOH** leads to $\beta$-elimination to form alkenes.
   - **Saytzeff's Rule (Zaitsev's rule)**: The more highly substituted alkene is the major product.
3. **Reaction with Metals**:
   - **Grignard Reagent**: $RX + Mg \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} RMgX$. (Must be anhydrous!).
   - **Wurtz Reaction**: $2RX + 2Na \xrightarrow{\text{dry ether}} R-R + 2NaX$.

### Chemical Properties of Haloarenes
Haloarenes are **less reactive** towards nucleophilic substitution than haloalkanes due to:
1. **Resonance effect**: C-X bond acquires partial double bond character.
2. **Difference in hybridization**: C is $sp^2$ hybridized (more electronegative, holds electron pair tightly).
3. **Instability of phenyl cation**.
4. **Electronic repulsion**: Nucleophile repelled by electron-rich arene ring.

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### $S_N1$ Mechanism (Unimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution)
Occurs in **two steps**. Favored by polar protic solvents.
- **Step 1 (Slow, Rate determining)**: Formation of carbocation.
  $R-X \rightleftharpoons R^+ + X^-$
- **Step 2 (Fast)**: Nucleophile attack.
  $R^+ + Nu^- \rightarrow R-Nu$
- **Stereochemistry**: Leads to **racemization** (formation of equal amounts of d and l isomers) because the planar carbocation can be attacked from both sides.
- **Order of reactivity**: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ$ (due to carbocation stability).

### $S_N2$ Mechanism (Bimolecular Nucleophilic Substitution)
Occurs in a **single step**. Favored by polar aprotic solvents.
- **Mechanism**: The nucleophile attacks the electrophilic carbon from the side opposite to the halogen. A transition state forms where C-Nu bond formation and C-X bond breaking occur simultaneously.
- **Stereochemistry**: Leads to **inversion of configuration** (Walden inversion).
- **Order of reactivity**: $1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$ (due to **steric hindrance**).

### ⚠️ ISC Traps & Subjective Pitfalls
- **Trap**: Forgetting the condition for Grignard reagent formation.
  - *Correction*: You MUST write "**dry ether**" over the arrow. Trace moisture converts Grignard to alkane: $RMgX + H_2O \rightarrow RH + Mg(OH)X$.
- **Trap**: Using Peroxide effect with HCl or HI.
  - *Correction*: Anti-Markovnikov addition (peroxide effect) occurs **only with HBr**.
- **Trap**: Writing $AgCN$ reaction yielding cyanide.
  - *Correction*: $KCN$ (ionic) yields **Alkyl Cyanide** ($R-CN$). $AgCN$ (covalent) yields **Alkyl Isocyanide** ($R-NC$). Similar for $KNO_2$ (nitrite, $R-O-N=O$) and $AgNO_2$ (nitro, $R-NO_2$).

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

### Q1. ISC Step-by-Step Problem: Name Reaction Conversion
**Convert Aniline to Chlorobenzene.**

**ISC Method:**
1. **Diazotization**: Treat aniline ($C_6H_5NH_2$) with $NaNO_2$ and $HCl$ at 0-5$^\circ$C.
   $C_6H_5NH_2 + NaNO_2 + 2HCl \xrightarrow{273-278 \text{ K}} C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- + NaCl + 2H_2O$
2. **Sandmeyer Reaction**: Treat the resulting benzene diazonium chloride with Cuprous chloride ($Cu_2Cl_2$) in HCl.
   $C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- \xrightarrow{Cu_2Cl_2 / HCl} C_6H_5Cl + N_2$

### Q2. ISC Reasoning Problem: Reactivity
**Out of chlorobenzene and benzyl chloride, which is more easily hydrolyzed by aqueous NaOH and why?**

**ISC Method:**
1. **Identify the compounds**: Chlorobenzene ($C_6H_5Cl$) is a haloarene. Benzyl chloride ($C_6H_5CH_2Cl$) is an aryl-alkyl halide.
2. **Explain Benzyl Chloride reactivity**: In benzyl chloride, the C-Cl bond is on an $sp^3$ carbon. Upon ionization, it forms a **benzyl carbocation** ($C_6H_5CH_2^+$), which is highly resonance stabilized. Thus it undergoes $S_N1$ easily.
3. **Explain Chlorobenzene inertness**: In chlorobenzene, the C-Cl bond acquires partial double bond character due to resonance. The carbon is $sp^2$ hybridized, making the bond shorter and stronger.
4. **Conclusion**: Benzyl chloride is hydrolyzed much more easily.
