# Chapter 14: Biomolecules

## 1. Exhaustive Theory & Precise ISC Terminology

Biomolecules are complex organic compounds which govern the common activities of living organisms. 

### Carbohydrates
Optically active polyhydroxy aldehydes or ketones, or compounds which produce them on hydrolysis.
- **Classification**:
  - **Monosaccharides**: Cannot be hydrolyzed further. (e.g., Glucose, Fructose, Ribose).
  - **Oligosaccharides**: Yield 2-10 monosaccharide units on hydrolysis. (e.g., Sucrose, Maltose, Lactose).
  - **Polysaccharides**: Yield a large number of monosaccharide units. (e.g., Starch, Cellulose, Glycogen).
- **Reducing vs Non-Reducing Sugars**:
  - *Reducing*: Carbohydrates that reduce Fehling's solution and Tollens' reagent. (All monosaccharides, maltose, lactose). Have free aldehyde/ketonic groups.
  - *Non-reducing*: Do not reduce them. (e.g., Sucrose - because the reducing groups of glucose and fructose are involved in glycosidic bond formation).

### Proteins
Polymers of **$\alpha$-amino acids** connected by **peptide bonds** (-CO-NH-).
- **Amino Acids**: Contain both $-NH_2$ (amino) and $-COOH$ (carboxyl) groups. They exist as **Zwitterions** (dipolar ions) in aqueous solution at a specific pH (Isoelectric point).
- **Classification of Proteins (Structure)**:
  - **Primary ($1^\circ$)**: Linear sequence of amino acids.
  - **Secondary ($2^\circ$)**: Folding of polypeptide chain ($\alpha$-helix and $\beta$-pleated sheet) due to **Hydrogen bonding**.
  - **Tertiary ($3^\circ$)**: Overall 3D shape, stabilized by H-bonds, disulfide linkages, van der Waals, and electrostatic forces.
  - **Quaternary ($4^\circ$)**: Spatial arrangement of multiple polypeptide chains (subunits).
- **Denaturation**: Loss of biological activity due to unfolding of $2^\circ$ and $3^\circ$ structure caused by heat, pH change, or chemicals. **Primary structure remains intact**. (e.g., coagulation of egg white).

### Vitamins
Organic compounds required in small amounts for normal growth and maintenance.
- **Fat Soluble**: A, D, E, K. (Stored in liver and adipose tissues).
- **Water Soluble**: B complex, C. (Must be supplied regularly in diet, excreted in urine. *Exception*: B12 is stored in liver).

### Nucleic Acids
Polymers of **Nucleotides** responsible for heredity and protein synthesis.
- **DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid)**: Sugar = $\beta$-D-2-deoxyribose. Bases = Adenine (A), Guanine (G), Cytosine (C), Thymine (T). Structure = Double helix.
- **RNA (Ribonucleic Acid)**: Sugar = $\beta$-D-ribose. Bases = A, G, C, Uracil (U). Structure = Single stranded.
- **Components**:
  - **Nucleoside**: Base + Sugar. (Linked via N-glycosidic linkage).
  - **Nucleotide**: Base + Sugar + Phosphate. (Linked via Phosphodiester linkage).

## 2. Step-by-Step Derivations & Mechanisms

### Evidence for Open Chain Structure of Glucose
To prove Glucose ($C_6H_{12}O_6$) is a straight-chain aldohexose:
1. **Prolonged heating with HI**: Forms **n-hexane**. *Conclusion*: All 6 carbons are linked in a straight chain.
2. **Reaction with $NH_2OH$ / HCN**: Forms oxime/cyanohydrin. *Conclusion*: Presence of a carbonyl ($>C=O$) group.
3. **Mild oxidation (Bromine water)**: Forms gluconic acid (a 6-carbon carboxylic acid). *Conclusion*: The carbonyl group is an **aldehyde**.
4. **Vigorous oxidation ($HNO_3$)**: Forms saccharic acid (a dicarboxylic acid). *Conclusion*: Presence of a primary alcoholic ($-CH_2OH$) group at the end.
5. **Acetylation with Acetic Anhydride**: Forms glucose pentaacetate. *Conclusion*: Presence of **five -OH groups** attached to different carbon atoms.

### Cyclic Structure of Glucose (Haworth Projection)
- **Checklist for $\alpha$-D-Glucopyranose**:
  - Draw a 6-membered pyranose ring (5 carbons, 1 oxygen).
  - Number carbons 1 to 5 clockwise starting right of oxygen. Carbon 6 is $-CH_2OH$ pointing up from C5.
  - $\alpha$-anomer: The -OH group on C1 (anomeric carbon) points **DOWN**.
  - Remaining -OH directions: C2 (down), C3 (up), C4 (down).
- **Checklist for $\beta$-D-Glucopyranose**:
  - Same as above, but the -OH on C1 points **UP**.

### ⚠️ ISC Traps & Subjective Pitfalls
- **Trap**: Forgetting the anomeric carbon.
  - *Correction*: The carbon atom involved in hemiacetal formation (C1 in glucose, C2 in fructose) that becomes an asymmetric center is called the **anomeric carbon**. Isomers differing only here are **anomers**.
- **Trap**: Thinking denaturation destroys the entire protein.
  - *Correction*: Denaturation only destroys the secondary and tertiary structures. The **primary structure (peptide bonds) is unaffected**.
- **Trap**: Confusing Glycosidic, Peptide, and Phosphodiester linkages.
  - *Correction*: 
    - Carbohydrates $\rightarrow$ **Glycosidic** (C-O-C)
    - Proteins $\rightarrow$ **Peptide** (-CO-NH-)
    - Nucleic Acids $\rightarrow$ **Phosphodiester** (C-O-P-O-C)

## 5. High-Yield Worked Model Problems

### Q1. ISC Subjective Problem: Vitamin Deficiencies
**Name the disease caused by the deficiency of Vitamin A, Vitamin C, and Vitamin D.**

**ISC Method:**
1. **Vitamin A**: Night blindness (Nyctalopia) and Xerophthalmia.
2. **Vitamin C**: Scurvy (bleeding gums).
3. **Vitamin D**: Rickets (in children - bone deformities) and Osteomalacia (in adults - soft bones).

### Q2. ISC Reasoning Problem:
**What is meant by inversion of sugar?**

**ISC Method:**
1. Sucrose is **dextrorotatory** ($+66.5^\circ$).
2. On hydrolysis with dilute acid or the enzyme invertase, it breaks down into an equimolar mixture of D-(+)-glucose ($+52.5^\circ$) and D-(-)-fructose ($-92.4^\circ$).
3. Since the laevorotation of fructose is greater than the dextrorotation of glucose, the resulting mixture is **laevorotatory**.
4. This change in the sign of optical rotation (from dextro to laevo) upon hydrolysis is called **inversion of sugar**, and the mixture is called **invert sugar**.
